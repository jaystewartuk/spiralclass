# D-112. The teaching language is asked at onboarding, not buried in settings; per-package Subject leaves the onboarding step (2026-08-08)

**Status: Active.** Completes [D-72](./D-72.md) (the platform is language-first;
`teachers.discipline` → `teachers.target_language`) by fixing where the field is
_asked for_. Constrained by [D-20](./D-20.md) (focus tags are teacher-owned
rows, seeded additively). Answers §3.4 of the 2026-08-05 pre-launch review.

**What changed.** Three calls, all about placement and defaults — **no new
column, no migration**:

1. **The account-wide teaching language is confirmed as the model, and it
   already exists.** It is `teachers.target_language`, a BCP-47 code from the
   234-language registry in `@spiralclass/shared`. The review that raised this
   described the teacher-level field as `account.discipline` and asked whether
   to repurpose it into a language picker; that repurposing was already done by
   D-72 on 2026-07-15, three weeks before the review. **The open question was
   never "should the field exist" — it is "why does nothing ask for it".**

2. **Onboarding asks for it, on the existing step 1.** The teaching language
   joins timezone / country / phone / pricing currency on
   `/onboarding/timezone` (`timezone-form.tsx`) rather than becoming a fifth
   step.
   Step 1 is already the "about you" step, and it already renders a 250-entry
   Combobox for country — the same component, the same shape of question, one
   more field. A new step would have added the friction this decision is
   supposed to remove. The settings copy at
   `/settings/booking-page` stays exactly where it is as the edit surface.

3. **Per-package `Subject` leaves the onboarding templates step and survives
   everywhere else.** The field disappears from
   `onboarding/templates/templates-form.tsx:336` **when that form is rendered
   inside onboarding**; the same component rendered at
   `/settings/templates` keeps it. `PackageTemplate.subject` is not deprecated:
   it stays a nullable free-text column, still written by the templates form,
   still read by the student package list and the package details sheet, where
   it is the
   only thing distinguishing "8 classes" from "8 classes" for a student who
   holds packages across two teachers.

**Why.** D-72 made `target_language` load-bearing in four places — the
focus-tag seed pack (`focus-packs.ts`), the subject noun in the AI material
prompt (`materials/prompt.ts`), the student-portal teacher profile, and the
teacher's own booking-page settings — and then left it reachable only from
Settings → Booking page → Teaching. Onboarding is timezone → availability →
packages → link; none of those four steps mention it. A teacher who completes
onboarding and starts teaching has `target_language = NULL`.

That null is not inert, and this is the part that decides the sequencing:
`getTeacherFocusTags()` **self-seeds on an empty read**, using whatever
`target_language` holds at that moment (`focus-tags.ts:167`). Null resolves to
the neutral `language` pack (`packKeyFor()` → `"language"`). So the first time
the teacher opens the focus picker, the generic pack is written to her account
permanently — and because seeding is additive by D-20, setting her real
language afterwards **appends** the `es`/`en` pack alongside the generic one
rather than replacing it. She ends up with two overlapping tag sets and no way
back short of archiving tags by hand. The cost of not asking is paid once, in a
place the teacher cannot easily undo, which is why the question has to land
before the first focus-picker render rather than "somewhere in settings".

The other half — dropping Subject from onboarding — is the friction trade the
review actually asked for. Asking a teacher of French to type "Conversation"
into four package rows before she has a single student is a worse first
five minutes than asking her once, on step 1, what language she teaches. And on
a language-first platform the answer to "what is this teacher's subject" is a
property of the teacher, not of each package: it feeds
`languageName(target_language)` in the prompt, the pack selection, and (later)
matching and SEO. Per-package subject remains the honest answer to a genuinely
different question — "which of my offerings is this" — for the minority who
sell more than one kind of course.

**What was affected.** Nothing yet: this decision records the call. The change
it authorizes touches `onboarding/timezone/*` and `onboarding/templates/*`,
`actions/onboarding.ts` (which currently reads `tpl_subject` at line 272), and
the `web.onboarding.templates.subject*` / `onboarding.templates.subject*`
catalog keys. The schema is untouched.

**Unresolved risks.**

- **Two write paths, one of which does not seed.** `saveTargetLanguageAction`
  (`actions/profile.ts:150`) calls `ensureTeacherFocusTags` after storing the
  language; a second write path sets `targetLanguage` and does **not**. The
  self-heal on first read covers it, but only because the read happens later —
  that path has correct tags by luck of ordering, not by design. The
  onboarding write must seed explicitly rather than inherit this asymmetry.
- **Existing teachers are not backfilled by this decision.** Anyone already
  past onboarding with `target_language = NULL` keeps the generic pack already
  written to their account. There is no migration here and no prompt in the
  dashboard telling them to go set it; whether that needs a one-time nudge is a
  separate call, not made here.
- **The field is called public catalog data but is not public.**
  `target-language-form.tsx:12` justifies its placement on the booking-page
  settings because "students browse and filter by it" — yet `b/[slug]`, the
  actual public booking page, never renders `targetLanguage`. It appears only
  on the signed-in student portal (`(student)/my-classes/teachers/*`). Putting
  the question in onboarding makes the field's absence from the public page
  more conspicuous, not less. Surfacing it there is unscoped follow-up.
- **Making it required is not decided.** This decision puts the question on
  step 1; it does not say whether step 1 refuses to advance without an answer.
  Required removes the null-pack problem outright and costs a teacher one
  Combobox; optional keeps onboarding forgiving and leaves the trap open for
  whoever skips. The implementing change has to pick one, and the null branches
  (`focusTagSeedFor(null)`, `prompt.ts`'s empty-target handling) stay either
  way — pre-D-112 teachers keep reaching them.
- **Dropping Subject is invisible in CI.** No gate runs the end-to-end device
  flows, so one still typing into `template-subject-*` fails days later and
  reads as flakiness rather than as this change.
