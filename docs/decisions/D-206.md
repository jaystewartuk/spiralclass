# D-206. A reader of a translation never sees the speaker's untranslated words; a phrase in progress shows as the speaker talking (2026-10-27)

**Status.** Active, built 2026-10-27. Narrows [D-198](./D-198.md).

**What changed.** While a phrase is still being spoken, the `partial` line
(D-198) carries the words heard so far **only when the reader reads the
speaker's own language**. When her subtitles are a translation, the partial
carries no words: `text` is empty and `lang` is the reader's language. The band
shows it as the speaker's name with three breathing dots, and the finished,
translated line replaces it under the same id, exactly as before. The in-call
transcript panel leaves wordless partials out, and it shows one language per
entry in the reader's display mode, the same rule the band follows.

**Why.** On 2026-10-26, after the build that removed the band's
original-above-translation mode went live (14:43 Mexico City time, Cloud Run
revision `web-00020`), a teacher and a student tested it in a class and both
found that the band still showed two languages and was still confusing. The
band was rendering one language per line, as intended. The second language
was the D-198 partial. Each phrase's untranslated words appeared under the
translation of the phrase before, because the band shows up to three lines.

D-198 rested on two premises. The first was that "in a language lesson the
words the speaker is saying are the lesson, and the band already shows the
original above the translation". That stopped being true when the one teacher
using that mode asked for it to go. The second was that a blank band while
someone talks reads as broken. That still holds, and the dots answer it
without a second language.

**What was considered and rejected.**

- **Hiding partials on the receiver.** The reader's browser does not know
  which language she reads until the first translated line arrives, and the
  words would still cross the wire to a screen that must not show them. The
  sender knows the direction, so the sender decides.
- **Nothing on screen until the translation lands** (before D-198). This is
  the half-minute silence a teacher reported on 2026-10-12.
- **Translating partials.** Already rejected by D-198, for cost and for
  half-translated text rewritten under the reader's eye.

**What it constrains.**

- A partial sent to a reader of a translation must carry no words. This is
  asserted in `packages/shared/src/caption-recognition.test.ts` and
  `apps/web/tests/captions/browser-captions.test.ts`.
- Neither the band nor the transcript panel puts two languages on screen for
  one reader. This is asserted in `apps/web/tests/video/caption-band.test.tsx`.

**What was affected.** `packages/shared/src/caption-recognition.ts`
(`buildPartialCaptionLine` takes `targetLanguage`),
`apps/web/src/lib/captions/browser-captions.ts`,
`apps/web/src/components/video/caption-band.tsx` (the dots, and the transcript
panel's display mode), `apps/web/src/components/video/class-call.tsx`, and
[live-calls-video.md](../features/live-calls-video.md).

**Trade-offs accepted, and unresolved risks.**

- **A reader of a translation still waits for the end of a phrase to read
  anything.** Now she sees who is talking while she waits. A long explanation
  is still one long wait, and nothing measures how long.
- **A reader of the same language is unchanged.** She still gets the words
  as they are spoken.
- **A tab opened before this deploy** renders a wordless partial as an empty
  line until the finished line replaces it.
- **"Original" display mode still works for finished lines only.** A reader
  testing herself on the original does not see it word by word any more.
