# D-19. AI lesson insights: async post-class pipeline, buy ASR + pronunciation, teacher-validated, derive-then-discard audio

> **Superseded in part by [D-87](./D-87.md) (2026-07-19).** The categorisation
> model named below (`claude-opus-4-8`) is no longer current — `INSIGHTS_MODEL`
> and `BRIEF_MODEL` are both `claude-haiku-4-5`, for cost. The buy-vs-build
> split, the teacher-in-the-loop gate, and the derive-then-discard audio policy
> are unchanged.

**Status: Accepted (design).** Direction agreed; unbuilt. The full design is the
sketch `../lesson-insights.md`; this doc pins the policy
calls so they're settled before code. Phases land incrementally (A–F there).

**What changed.** The in-class call's purpose is made explicit: it is the
**data-capture pipeline** for AI-driven, longitudinal language-learning insights
— per-student focus areas (pronunciation, grammar, vocabulary, fluency) that
accumulate across lessons and tell teacher and student what to work on next. This
reframes owning the call (D-16) and recording (the call-recording follow-up) as
means to that end, and makes the existing post-class `lesson_summaries` the
embryo of a **structured, accumulating, teacher-validated** profile. A Pro
capability (D-15).

**What was decided.**

1. **Process asynchronously after class, not in real time.** Analysis runs on the
   existing Inngest rail once the lesson audio is ready. No real-time AI during
   the call — lower cost, no latency pressure, and re-runnable as models improve.
2. **Record per-participant audio tracks.** LiveKit Egress records the student's
   mic as its own object. Because the student's identity is known from the LiveKit
   grant, an isolated track gives **perfect speaker attribution (zero diarization
   error)**. Audio-only per-participant egress is the analysis input; a full A/V
   recording stays a separate, optional artifact for human playback only.
3. **Buy the commodity ML; build the rest.** ASR + word timestamps from
   **Deepgram or AssemblyAI**; pronunciation assessment from **Azure AI Speech**;
   categorisation from **Claude (`claude-opus-4-8`)**, reusing the
   degrade-gracefully pattern of `lesson_summaries`. We build the data model, the
   teacher-validation loop, the longitudinal profile, and the UX — the moat. We do
   **not** build ASR or pronunciation scoring in-house.
4. **Teacher-in-the-loop is the quality gate.** AI _proposes_ focus areas; a
   finding only counts toward the profile once the teacher confirms/edits it
   (`confirmedAt`). The teacher is a labeller, not a reviewer of an autonomous
   system. This closes the loop: the live-notes cue mechanism is **both** the
   input (teacher labels/corrections) **and** the output (next lesson's
   AI-suggested cues).
5. **Every finding is timestamp-anchored.** Each insight carries the student's
   actual utterance and its moment in the recording, so it's playable and
   reviewable — never an unverifiable assertion.
6. **Privacy: derive then discard (default).** Once the transcript + scores +
   profile exist, the **raw audio is deleted** unless the user explicitly opts to
   keep it for playback. We keep the low-sensitivity derivatives (text + scores +
   profile) and shed the high-sensitivity asset (voice, possibly a minor's). A
   visible recording indicator, a stated retention window, consent, and delete are
   all in scope.

**Why.**

- _Owning the call earns its keep here:_ D-16's overlay thesis is real, but the
  larger payoff is that owning the media is the only way to get the audio that
  powers a learning profile. This decision is what makes D-16 strategic rather
  than just a nicer call window.
- _Per-participant audio over diarization:_ trusting a diarizer to separate two
  Spanish speakers is a quality risk on the most important signal (the student's
  speech). Recording the student's track directly removes the risk entirely.
- _Teacher-in-the-loop over autonomous AI:_ raw LLM error-detection on L2 speech
  is noisy; shipping unvalidated "you have a grammar problem" erodes trust fast.
  Validation buys accuracy _and_ a labelled dataset _and_ trust, and it reuses a
  surface the teacher already touches.
- _Derive-then-discard:_ the learning value lives in the derivatives, not the
  waveform. Holding a minor's voice recording indefinitely is the liability we
  don't need to carry to deliver the product.

**What it constrains.**

- Don't add real-time/in-call AI analysis — keep it post-class on Inngest unless a
  specific feature (e.g. live captions) justifies a separate real-time path.
- Don't build ASR or pronunciation scoring; integrate a vendor behind a seam (mirror
  the `VideoProvider` pattern so the vendor is swappable).
- Don't persist raw lesson audio past processing without an explicit user opt-in
  and a retention policy; transcripts/insights are teacher-only (no student-visible
  row exposes a raw transcript), §13.2 + owner RLS like `lesson_summaries`.
- Insights are Pro-gated (D-15); a finding without `confirmedAt` must not feed the
  profile or any student-facing output.

**Unresolved risks.**

- **ASR vendor + target-language scope** — Deepgram vs AssemblyAI; pronunciation
  coverage (Azure) is the binding constraint on which languages launch. Likely
  Spanish-first.
- **Accuracy & teacher burden** — too many low-quality AI proposals turns the
  validation step into a chore; the analysis prompt and confidence thresholds must
  keep proposals few and high-signal.
- **Cost** — ASR (~per-minute), pronunciation (per-utterance), and LLM per lesson;
  sized per Pro lesson, but watch it as volume grows.
- **Per-participant egress cost vs the full recording** — whether audio-only
  egress supplements or replaces the A/V recording is a cost/playback trade-off to
  settle in Phase A.
