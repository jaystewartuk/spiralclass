# D-195. One locale per language until a region earns its own, and a regional locale is a delta over its language, never a copy (2026-10-04)

**Status.** Decided, not built. Nothing is built until a second locale of one
language is wanted.

**What changed.** No regional split now:

- `es` stays one locale, written as Latin American Spanish. It is formatted
  and handed to Stripe as `es-419`, which is what the registry already says.
- Portuguese, when it comes (#76), ships as `pt-BR`. Brazil is where the
  evidence is, and Brazilian Portuguese is what that market reads.

When a region does earn its own locale:

- **It is a delta.** A regional locale (`es-ES`, `pt-PT`, `fr-CA`) is a
  registry row whose catalog holds **only the keys whose wording differs**. A
  key it lacks resolves through its base: `es-ES` → `es` → `en`.
- **The base** is the first locale registered for that language.
- **Fallback never goes sideways.** It never crosses into another language
  (`pt-PT` never falls to `es`). Like every other fallback here, it ends at
  `DEFAULT_LOCALE`, never Spanish.
- **Browser matching** stays by language. `Accept-Language: es-ES` gets `es`
  today, and gets `es-ES` once that exists. A region with no locale of its own
  gets its language's base.

**Why.** A split doubles the translation, the native review and the guard
patterns for a language, and nothing measured asks for it. The platform's
Spanish-reading teachers and buyers are in Mexico and Latin America. The
catalog is written for them: it has no `vosotros` forms, and "profe" appears
104 times. A reader in Spain can read it, and nothing has shown that one has
tried. Brazilian Portuguese is the
reverse case. #76's traffic evidence is Brazilian, and Brazilian and European
Portuguese differ enough in vocabulary, address and spelling that whichever
is written first should be the one the market reads.

The delta rule is the long-term part. A full regional copy means 5,945 keys
kept in step with a near-identical sibling forever. Most of those keys would
be identical, and the ones that drift would drift by accident. A delta holds
only the differences, so every difference is deliberate and reviewable.

**What was considered and rejected.**

- **Split `es` into `es-MX` and `es-ES` now.** The cost is real (a second
  catalog, a second reviewer) and the benefit is unmeasured.
- **Ship plain `pt` and let readers in either country cope.** It still has to
  be written in one variety, so calling it `pt` hides a choice rather than
  avoiding one. `Intl` already treats plain `pt` as Brazilian
  (`€ 1.234,50`, against `pt-PT`'s `1234,50 €`). The tag would say "Portuguese"
  while the formatting says Brazil.
- **A full catalog per regional locale.** See above. The compile-time
  completeness guard (D-52) would demand it, so a delta locale needs its own
  type: a partial table over its base, checked against the base's keys.

**What it constrains.**

- Do not register a second locale for a language as a full catalog. Build the
  delta type first.
- A regional locale's fallback chain is its base, then `DEFAULT_LOCALE`.
  Nothing else.
- The `intl` and Stripe values on a registry row are the region that locale
  is written for (`es` → `es-419`). Changing them changes how every number,
  date and payment page reads for that language, which needs D-197's tests to
  move with it.

**What was affected.** Nothing yet. The future delta touches:

- `packages/shared/src/i18n/locales.ts`: a `base` field on a row.
- `packages/shared/src/i18n/translate.ts`: the lookup chain.
- The catalog's completeness type.
- `matchAcceptLanguage`: prefer a regional match when one exists.

**Trade-offs accepted, and unresolved risks.**

- **A reader in Spain reads Latin American Spanish.** That includes the
  register of address, and "profe" where Spain says "profesor". Accepted until
  demand shows otherwise.
- **The catalog's register is not written down anywhere.** Whether `es`
  addresses the reader as `tú` or `usted`, and which words are Mexican rather
  than pan-Latin-American, lives only in the strings. The per-language
  glossary in #178 step D is where it gets written down, and a delta needs it
  before it can say what differs.
