# D-183. The Oracle tenancy becomes Pay As You Go, and everything stays inside Always Free

**Status.** Active · narrows [D-150](./D-150.md)'s near-zero-cost premise
rather than reversing it, and does not by itself return production video to a
box — [D-182](./D-182.md)'s three conditions still govern that.

**What changed.** The OCI tenancy's home region is unchanged
(`mx-queretaro-1`); its account type was upgraded from Free Tier to **Pay As
You Go** on 2026-09-20. No resource changed and no recurring bill starts:
Oracle does not charge for Always Free resources after an upgrade, and
`infra/oracle-box/` is sized exactly at the Always Free ceilings. A monthly
budget of 1 USD named `always-free-tripwire` alerts at 1% actual spend, which
is the first cent.

**Why.** `CUTOVER.md` step 3 destroyed the box on 2026-09-19 and every A1
launch since was refused `Out of host capacity` — 56 of them between 13:38 and
22:14, in a region with one availability domain, against a tenancy whose own
limits were clear. Oracle's [Free Tier FAQ](https://www.oracle.com/cloud/free/faq/)
states that a paid account keeps every Always Free benefit at no cost **and**
receives priority for launching instances. So this buys capacity priority for
nothing, which no other option on the board does.

The sizing is not an assumption. Measured against the tenancy on 2026-09-20,
after the terminate and before any apply:

| Limit                      | Available | Used |
| -------------------------- | --------- | ---- |
| `total-storage-gb`         | 200       | 0    |
| `standard-a1-core-count`   | 2         | 0    |
| `standard-a1-memory-count` | 12        | 0    |

Taken with `oci limits resource-availability get --availability-domain
benl:MX-QUERETARO-1-AD-1`. The zero in the right-hand column is the part worth
keeping: no orphaned boot volume survived the terminate, which under Free Tier
would have failed the apply on the storage limit and under Pay As You Go would
instead have billed quietly.

**What was considered and rejected.** Each of these costs money **and** an
architecture change, where the upgrade costs neither:

- **Hetzner CAX21** (ARM, €10.49/mo), the graduation target
  [D-94](./D-94.md) names. Hetzner sells ARM only in Germany and Finland, which
  puts roughly 160-190 ms on the media path between two people who are both in
  the Americas. Acceptable for preview and its Postgres, wrong for the SFU.
- **Hetzner Ashburn CPX22** (€19.49/mo) or **Vultr's nearest Latin American region** (~$24/mo).
  Both are x86, and `scripts/oracle-push-images.sh` fails closed on a
  non-arm64 image — so either one reintroduces the amd64 cross-build under QEMU
  that D-150 counted as a gain of moving here in the first place.
- **A different Oracle region.** Already rejected in D-182: a new VCN, a new
  reserved address and new DNS, for the same capacity lottery and worse
  latency.
- **Retrying alone.** The loop still runs, but it is a lottery with no stated
  odds, and D-182 exists because losing it has a cost.

**What it constrains.** **The failure mode of every Always Free ceiling has
inverted.** Under Free Tier, asking for more than 2 OCPUs, 12 GB or 200 GB was
_refused by the service limit_. Under Pay As You Go the same request is
_billed_. `infra/oracle-box/variables.tf` sits exactly on all three, so
`ocpus`, `memory_in_gbs` and `boot_volume_gb` may not be raised as a
troubleshooting step — raising one is now a decision to spend money, and it
fails silently in the direction of a bill rather than loudly in the direction
of an error.

The reserved public IPv4 is free **while attached to a live VNIC**. A box
destroyed again, with the address left floating, is the one line item that
starts counting while nobody is looking.

**What was affected.** No application code and no Terraform resource. The
comments that stated the old failure mode were corrected in
`infra/oracle-box/variables.tf` and `infra/oracle-box/README.md`, both of which
said in as many words that a launch is rejected rather than billed.
`docs/deployment/COST_PLAYBOOK.md`'s graduation line was repriced at the same
time, having named a Hetzner plan that no longer exists.

**Trade-offs accepted, and unresolved risks.**

- **Capacity priority is Oracle's claim, not a measurement.** Nothing here
  proves the box comes back, and paid tenancies have waited days for A1
  elsewhere. D-182 stays in force until a real class has been joined on a box.
- **A card is now on file against a tenancy whose entire design is to cost
  nothing.** The budget is a smoke alarm, not a fuse: Oracle budgets notify and
  do not cap, and OCI has no hard spend limit to set.
- **What it removes, in exchange:** Oracle documents reclaiming _idle_ Always
  Free compute instances. A paid tenancy is not subject to that, which retires
  one of the two operational risks [D-49](./D-49.md) held against putting
  anything prod-critical on this box. The other — no SLA — is untouched.
- **Nothing in this tree can check either fact.** No test can read the account
  tier or the budget, so this record and the OCI console are the only place
  both are written down. If the account is ever downgraded, the guidance above
  inverts again and nothing will say so.
