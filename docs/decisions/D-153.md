# D-153. Money is integer minor units in the price's own recorded currency, and only the number format follows the reader

Every amount is stored as integer minor units in a `*_minor_units` column next
to the currency it is in, and is converted and formatted using that currency's
exponent (`currencyExponent()` in
[money.ts](../../packages/shared/src/money.ts)), never a hardcoded 100. A
teacher prices her lessons in one currency she picks at onboarding
(`teachers.pricing_currency`, validated against the curated list in
[pricing-currency.ts](../../packages/shared/src/pricing-currency.ts)). The
platform bills its own subscriptions in GBP (`PLATFORM_MONEY_CURRENCY`).
`formatMinorUnits` writes a price with the reader's separators and grouping,
but always in the price's own currency, with the ISO code shown exactly once.

## Why

The platform serves teachers and students in any country. That means many
currencies, eight of which have no hundredth part (JPY, KRW, CLP, VND, PYG,
UGX, XAF, XOF). Two mistakes are easy to make:

- Assuming a currency's exponent. The entry side once multiplied by a
  hardcoded 100 while the display side divided by the real exponent, so a
  20,000 CLP price was stored as 2,000,000.
- Treating amounts in different currencies as one number. Adding up raw minor
  units across currencies gives a figure that means nothing.

## Options considered

- **`centavos` names** (the original columns) — for a zero-decimal currency
  they name a unit that does not exist, and they invite `/ 100` in new code.
- **`cents`** — familiar, and Stripe uses it, but it is wrong for the same
  eight currencies. _Minor unit_ is ISO 4217's own term, and
  `MINOR_UNIT_EXPONENTS` already used it.
- **Deriving the teacher's currency from the platform region** (MXN for
  everyone until July 2026) — mixes up what she sells in with what the
  platform's own Stripe entity settles in.
- **A Postgres enum or CHECK listing the allowed currencies** — every new
  currency would need a migration. The list lives in shared code
  (`isPricingCurrencySupported`). The database only checks the format: three
  uppercase letters.
- **Letting a teacher change her currency herself** — her existing packages,
  prices and payments are in the old currency, and a switch would corrupt that
  history. A change is a support request, like a change of country.
- **A different list per payout rail** (GBP, USD and EUR for card teachers) —
  correct while card money passed through a GBP balance, wrong once her own
  account settles in her own currency.
- **Showing subscription prices in several currencies** (Stripe
  `currency_options`) — means maintaining FX inside Stripe, handling tax per
  presentment currency, and keeping more Price objects in sync. One GBP price,
  converted by the card issuer, is enough.
- **Charging buyers in USD on an English booking page** — her Stripe account
  settles locally and her prices are fixed in her own currency. The funnel
  instead shows an approximate USD figure next to her price, for display only.
- **Formatting every price as `en-US`** — French readers see `$25,000 CLP` as
  twenty-five pesos.
- **Formatting in the currency's home locale** — a French reader would see a
  German `1.234,50 €`.
- **Dropping the code suffix when the symbol looks clear** — `$` alone
  stands for a dozen currencies.

## What it cost

- **The column rename cannot be undone by rolling back code.** The old code
  queries columns that no longer exist, so a rollback needs the database's
  pre-migration checkpoint. Admin CSV headers changed (`net_minor_units`,
  `payable_minor_units`, `amount_minor_units`), so spreadsheets keyed on the
  old names need updating.
- **Subscribers from before the GBP switch stay on MXN** until they renew,
  and admin totals report them in a separate bucket. Subscriptions created
  under the old Stripe entity could not be managed after the key swap.
- **The GBP prices were a first cut:** £7.99 monthly, £79.90 annual, £5.99
  founding. They were sized from the MXN→GBP rate in the economics
  assumptions (0.04249 as of 2026-07-15) and rounded.
- **One price can be written two ways.** A French teacher with an English
  booking page sees `1 500,00 $ MXN` while her buyers see `$1,500.00 MXN`.
  Tests compare against `Intl`'s real output, so they change when Node's ICU
  changes.

## What it constrains

- **Give the currency on both sides of every conversion.** On the server use
  `currencyForTeacher(teacher)`, and on the client the
  `PricingCurrencyProvider` context. If a new curated currency's exponent is
  not 2, add it to `MINOR_UNIT_EXPONENTS` in the same change. Guarded by
  [money.test.ts](../../packages/shared/src/money.test.ts) ("never repeats
  the currency code, for any currency a teacher can price in") and
  [templates-form-zero-decimal.test.ts](../../apps/web/tests/onboarding/templates-form-zero-decimal.test.ts).
- **Never add up minor units across currencies.** Total each currency
  separately, or convert through an explicit, dated rate.
  `money-metrics.ts` and `subscriptions/admin-metrics.ts` partition by
  currency. Guarded in
  [money-metrics.test.ts](../../apps/web/tests/lib/money-metrics.test.ts) and
  [admin-metrics.test.ts](../../apps/web/tests/subscriptions/admin-metrics.test.ts).
- **A stored row keeps the currency it was written in.** A subscriber's
  billing currency is read from the live Stripe object (`sub.currency` in
  `billing-webhook-handler.ts`), never re-derived from the region registry.
  `PLATFORM_MONEY_CURRENCY` only applies to a new checkout.
- **Add to the curated list; never remove from it.** A teacher already priced
  in a removed currency would have no valid value to move to.
- **Formatting is display only.** Never parse a formatted price back into an
  amount. Never use the reader's locale to choose or convert a currency.
  `formatMinorUnits` must be passed a locale, except in the allow-listed
  English-only places. Guarded by
  [money-locale.test.ts](../../apps/web/tests/i18n/money-locale.test.ts).
- **Where the code and the record differ, the code wins.** The pricing
  currency is "locked" only because the onboarding stepper never comes back to
  that page. The onboarding action writes `pricingCurrency` whenever a value is
  sent, and the server does not refuse a later change.

## How we got here

- **July 2026:** every teacher had been priced in MXN, because
  `currencyForTeacher()` read the only platform region. A production query
  (`select country, count(*) from teachers group by country`) returned two
  teachers, both in MX. The decision added a teacher-chosen
  `pricing_currency`, set once at onboarding. Card teachers could choose GBP,
  USD or EUR. Wise teachers could choose six Latin American currencies, a bet
  on the growth market with no usage data behind it. The Wise reconciler
  stopped reading one platform-wide balance currency and started reading each
  teacher's own currency.
- **July 2026:** platform subscription pricing moved from MXN to GBP to match
  the UK entity (migration dated 2026-07-22; the live GBP Prices were fixed on
  2026-07-27). Only defaults changed, and no stored subscription or invoice
  currency was rewritten. The audit found the billing webhook was taking the
  currency from the region config instead of the Stripe subscription. It also
  found MRR and subscription revenue adding MXN and GBP together, and package
  JSON-LD hardcoding `priceCurrency: "MXN"`. All three were fixed.
- **2026-08-23:** the manual-rail list grew from six currencies to about fifty,
  so that a teacher paid by local bank transfer could price in her own money.
- **2026-08-30:** under direct charges both rails offer the same list,
  because her own account settles in her own currency. Today there are 61
  curated currencies.
- **After that (undated in the original record):** eighteen columns across
  thirteen tables were renamed from `*_centavos` to `*_minor_units`, along with
  the money helpers (`formatMinorUnits`, `majorToMinorUnits`,
  `minorUnitsToMajor`) and constants. That was roughly 2,000 occurrences in
  258 files, done as a pure `RENAME COLUMN` migration. Comments that really
  are about MXN still say centavos.
- **2026-10-04:** formatting stopped being `en-US` for everyone. Measured with
  Node 24's ICU using `money.ts`'s own rule: Spanish (`es-419`) and English
  output was byte-identical to before, and only French changed (`25 000 $ CLP`,
  `1 234,50 € EUR`). The same work found the referral-reward email formatting
  its amount with no currency, which fell back to MXN.
