# D-202. The decision log keeps one record per live decision, edited in place

Each live decision has exactly one record, and that record is rewritten when the
decision changes; a record that a later one absorbed or that only described
behaviour is deleted, and its number is listed in the README's
[Retired numbers](./README.md#retired-numbers) table with where its reasoning
went. Numbers are still never reused or renumbered.

## Why

This reverses the log's two oldest rules: that a superseded record stays,
marked, and that a record is never edited. Their premise was that the
reasoning trail is the point, and that the reader is the author six weeks
later, who can hold the whole log in mind and follow a chain of banners.

That premise stopped being true. Measured on the starting commit with
`wc -w docs/decisions/D-*.md`, the log was **148 records and 147,413 words**.
Most records were links in chains five or six long, each carrying stacked
"Superseded in part by…" banners, so finding what is live meant reading every
link. Because records were never edited, titles went on stating things that
did not happen: the record that measured the app staying beside its database
was titled as if the app had moved. The first two records were reconstructed
afterwards and said so ("Why it likely changed"). The `decision-scout` agent,
whose job is to answer "what does this forbid?", spent its context reading
history to get there.

Git already keeps every version of every record, and unlike a directory of
superseded files it cannot be mistaken for current policy — the same reasoning
[D-164](./D-164.md) applies to runbooks.

## Options considered

- **Keep the log append-only and add a better index.** Lost because the index
  was already the problem: a reader still had to open the chain to find which
  link was live, and the scout still had to read it all.
- **Delete superseded records and renumber.** Lost because numbers are cited
  from outside the tree: applied migrations cite 39 different numbers and
  cannot be edited (Prisma checksums them), and pull-request descriptions cite
  58 (the maintainer's count when this was planned). A renumbered log would
  make every one of those point at the wrong decision, silently.
- **Fold chains into their live record and retire the rest by number** — taken.
  A retired number resolves through one table row, so an old citation still
  leads somewhere true.

## What it cost

Each surviving record was rewritten, and its reversals now live in a short
**How we got here** section instead of in the records that made them. The
original wording of every record is one click away at the
[starting commit](https://github.com/jaystewartuk/spiralclass/tree/1180a397eb16f961c02054920dc1603c77407647/docs/decisions),
but it is no longer in the tree a session reads. Citations across the code and
docs were repointed to the surviving record or rewritten to state the fact.

## What it constrains

- **Amend the live record for a topic; do not open a successor.** Put what
  changed, and why, in its How we got here section.
- **A new record has to clear a bar:** at least two real options, a wrong
  choice that is expensive or hard to undo, and a plausible future change that
  would undo it without knowing why. Otherwise the reasoning goes in the PR
  body, behaviour in `docs/features/`, and the constraint in a guard. The bar
  is in [`_template.md`](./_template.md).
- **Never reuse or renumber a number**, and never remove a row from the
  Retired numbers table: a migration may cite it forever.
- **Outside applied migrations, cite only live records.**
  [`decision-records.test.ts`](../../apps/web/tests/config/decision-records.test.ts)
  enforces this, that the table agrees with the tree, and a limit of 1,500
  words per record.

## How we got here

The log was published append-only, with 51 records curated out before
publication and dated forward pointers added to the head of reversed ones.
Over the following months the pointers became the main thing a reader saw. In
October 2026 the log was consolidated to one record per live decision under
this record.
