# D-147. The platform's regulatory position — kept privately

**Date:** 2026-08-31. **Content removed 2026-09-05, before publication
([D-159](./D-159.md)).**

**What this record covered.** The platform's position under three regimes: UK
digital platform reporting, payment services regulation, and Strong Customer
Authentication. It changed no code.

**Why it is not published.** It is the operator's regulatory and tax position,
not an engineering decision — see [D-159](./D-159.md). A repository that is
public for the engineering does not need to carry the state of one person's
obligations, and a reader looking for how this platform was built loses nothing
by its absence.

⚠️ **It is removed, not reversed.** The research stands, the obligations it
identified are tracked outside this repository, and nothing in the code depends
on it.

**The one finding that was engineering, kept because a reader of
[D-143](./D-143.md) will want it:** under direct charges, Strong Customer
Authentication is determined by the **connected account's** country rather than
the platform's. `on_behalf_of` exists to attribute a _destination_ charge to a
connected account; a direct charge is already on hers and needs no attribution,
so the workaround D-143 listed as unresolved is not needed — the problem it
solves does not arise.

**One consequence of that remains, and it is UX rather than compliance.** Under
direct charges the Customer and the saved payment method live on the teacher's
connected account, so a student buying from two teachers saves a card twice.
That is inherent to the funds flow and is not a defect to fix.
