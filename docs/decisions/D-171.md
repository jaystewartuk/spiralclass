# D-171. The audience is teachers of any subject; the schema stays language-shaped

**Status: Active.** Supersedes the _positioning_ call in [D-72](./D-72.md) —
"SpiralClass is a tool for **language teachers**" — and nothing else in that
record: its architecture (one registry, `teachers.target_language` as a BCP-47
code, `asr` a capability flag on it) is untouched and still describes the code.
Builds on the framing half of [D-133](./D-133.md), which made `CLAUDE.md`'s
opening the canonical statement of who this is for and which
[D-138](./D-138.md) left standing when it superseded only D-133's naming half.

⚠️ **Written after the fact.** The change it records was made in the copy and
guarded by a test before this repository was published, and the squashed
publication history does not carry its date. What follows is reconstructed from
the tree, not recalled: every claim below is checkable against a file named
beside it. It is written now because the log said "language teachers" while the
front door said "any subject", and a reader had no way to tell which was
current.

**What changed.** Every public marketing surface sells to **independent
teachers of any subject**. `landing.subhead`, `web.manifest.description`,
`web.og.sub`, `web.og.subWithStripe`, `web.layout.meta.description`,
`web.features.meta.description` and `web.features.sub` all name the subject and
none names a language, in all three locales. The repository's own front door
says the same thing (`README.md`, `CLAUDE.md`), as does the GitHub description.
**No schema changed, no migration ran, and no code branch was added** — a
teacher's subject is still a code from the 234-language registry.

**Why it likely changed.** The landing eyebrow read "For teachers of any
language" while the manifest, the Open Graph subtitle, the layout description
and the features headline beside it already said "any subject". One page made
two different promises about who the product is for, and the fix had to pick
one.

Subject is the one that survives, because it is the one the funnel can keep.
What the landing page actually sells — a booking link, prepaid packages, card
and Wise rails, automatic reminders, a calendar — has nothing to do with
languages. D-72's case for narrowing was never about those: it was that live
captions with ASR and translation, CEFR levels and a
grammar/vocabulary/skill taxonomy differentiate for language teachers and are
"worth nothing to a guitar teacher". That premise did not expire, and this
record does not touch it. What it corrects is where the claim was made: the
language-shaped features sit further inside the product than the funnel does,
so scoping the **audience** to language teaching gave away teachers the funnel
would have served, to protect features they would never have reached.

**What was considered and rejected.**

- **Narrow the copy back to "any language"** — the eyebrow's version, and the
  option that agrees with D-72 as written. Rejected because a music or a maths
  teacher reading "any language" reads herself out of a product whose booking,
  payment and reminder story fits her exactly, and because it would have meant
  editing four strings that already said "subject" to match one that did not.
- **Widen `teachers.target_language` in the same change**, to free text or to a
  subject registry. Rejected on cost and on reversibility: it is a migration, a
  registry, a picker and a prompt rewrite, with no teacher waiting on it, while
  every reason D-72 gave for storing a code (derived labels, one list, `asr` as
  a capability flag rather than a second list) still holds. A copy change can be
  reversed in one commit; that one cannot.

**What it constrains.**

- **Public marketing must not rescope the audience to language teaching
  again.** `packages/shared/src/i18n/i18n.test.ts` fails the build on eight
  patterns — a language word next to a teacher noun, in English, Spanish or
  French — across the eight public key prefixes. Rephrase the marketing freely;
  the guard is on the promise, not the wording.
- **This is not licence to treat `target_language` as free text.** The schema is
  deliberately narrower than the copy and both halves are true at once.
  Widening the column is its own change and needs its own record.
- **The funnel, the payment rails and the reminders stay subject-neutral.** They
  are what the promise rests on; a subject-specific assumption added to any of
  them makes the marketing false rather than merely optimistic.

**What was affected.** The three i18n catalogs in `packages/shared/src/i18n/`,
the guard in `i18n.test.ts`, `CLAUDE.md`'s opening and its "Product framing"
invariant, `README.md`, and the repository description on GitHub. Nothing under
`apps/web/src/lib/` and nothing in `prisma/`.

**Trade-offs accepted, and unresolved risks.**

- **The product under the copy is still language-shaped, and a non-language
  teacher meets that on her first day.** Onboarding **requires** a teaching
  language before she can finish — `timezone-form.tsx` blocks submit on an empty
  `targetLanguage` and the server action enforces the same rule — and the picker
  offers 234 languages and no other kind of subject. A maths teacher has no true
  answer to give it. This is the sharpest cost of the widening and nothing
  currently softens it.
- **What she gets if she answers anyway.** Every branch of
  `focusTagSeedFor()` seeds a language pack (`apps/web/src/lib/focus-packs.ts`)
  — English and Spanish have their own, everything else including an unset
  value gets the neutral one — so her starting focus tags are "Verb tenses",
  "Pronunciation", "Listening" and "Vocabulary" whatever she picks; and
  whichever code she picked is then fed to `buildMaterialPrompt` as the subject
  itself — "The teacher teaches X. Everything you produce must be X teaching
  material: this is an X lesson" (`apps/web/src/lib/materials/prompt.ts`) — so a
  maths teacher who answers "English" to get through onboarding has every
  generated material become an English lesson.
- **Nothing measures the gap**, because no teacher of a non-language subject is
  live. It stays theoretical until the first signup the marketing brings in, and
  that signup is the event that turns this from a copy decision into a product
  one.
- **D-72's title still reads "The platform is language-first."** Records here are
  not edited to match what came after them; its status line points at this one.
- **What would reopen this**: serving non-language subjects properly — a subject
  that is not a language code, a focus pack that is not a language pack, and a
  prompt that does not assume a language is being taught. That is the change
  this record deliberately did not make.
