# D-137. The platform stays out of the money flow — a card rail outside the Connect circle is the TEACHER's, never ours

**Status: Decided, nothing built (2026-08-28).** This doc ships no code. It
exists because "just add a card rail for Mexico" is an idea that will be
re-derived every few months by anyone who looks at the manual rail and sees a
conversion problem, and because the reason not to do it the obvious way is not
visible anywhere in the codebase.

**What changed.** The manual rail's defining property — **the platform never
touches the funds**, stated in [D-113](./D-113.md) as a description of how the
rail happens to work — is promoted to a **constraint that governs every future
payments decision**. Any rail in which AgendaProfe collects a student's money
and forwards it to a teacher outside the Stripe Connect circle
([D-58](./D-58.md)) is **out of scope** unless a future decision explicitly
reopens the regulatory question that closes it.
The option that IS in scope is named here: make **the teacher's own payee
instrument card-capable**, as rows in the existing manual-rail registry
([D-124](./D-124.md)).

## Why

**The question was asked from data that does not exist.** The trigger was a
reasonable worry that students will not complete a bank transfer to a teacher
they have never met. The checkout funnel at the time was almost entirely seed
and internal traffic, at a volume far too low to carry a conversion statistic.
**There is no rail-conversion statistic here, and there will not be one for a
while.** Any number quoted about this rail's conversion, in either direction, is
noise.

**Collecting the money is the trigger.** The regulatory analysis behind that —
which regimes engage on either side of this corridor, and what each would
require of the operator — is not published here; see [D-159](./D-159.md). What
survives it is the engineering constraint: **a rail in which the platform
collects a student's money and forwards it on is out of scope**, and the
Connect circle is not a Stripe quirk to route around.

**Every vendor that looks like the answer fails on the same point.**

- **Stripe Global Payouts** is the near miss, and the one most likely to be
  proposed again: the sending platform may be in Great Britain, and it reaches
  more than 160 countries. But its own documentation says the sender must
  "manage your own legal and compliance requirements", possibly including a
  money transmission licence. It **hands over the problem Connect absorbs**,
  and it does not change the Mexican analysis at all.
- **Mercado Pago split payments** is the closest thing to a Connect for Latin
  America — funds land in the teacher's own Mercado Pago account and the
  platform takes an application fee by OAuth. But it needs a marketplace
  account with local standing, and taking a fee out of a Mexican transaction is
  a **stronger** claim to being an intermediation platform, not a weaker one.
- **dLocal and EBANX** solve local acquiring without a Mexican entity by acting
  as merchant of record — meaning they collect, which returns to both problems
  above — at roughly 3 to 7 percent all in, with enterprise-shaped onboarding.
  Wrong scale for one teacher.

**Card is not automatically the higher-converting option in this corridor.**
Domestic card authorisation runs about 95 to 99 percent; cross-border commonly
fails 15 to 25 percent, because the issuer scores a foreign acquirer as
riskier. Meanwhile account-to-account transfers and wallets are roughly 46
percent of Latin American e-commerce, card penetration is comparatively low,
and about 70 percent of Latin American consumers are less willing to buy from a
site that omits local methods. **A Mexican student paying a Mexican teacher by
SPEI is doing an ordinary thing.** The trust deficit is sharp for a FOREIGN
student wiring money to an unfamiliar Mexican account, and that is the segment
any fix should be aimed at — not the domestic one.

## What is in scope instead

Make the teacher's own instrument card-capable. Each of these is a registry row
in the model D-124 already built, keeps the invariant, needs no new entity and
no licence of the platform's own, and reuses the existing instructions,
reference, self-attestation and teacher-confirmation flow unchanged:

- **A Wise payment link that accepts cards.** Wise Business can issue a link
  the payer settles by card with no Wise account of their own — 1 percent for a
  personal card issued in the United Kingdom, 2.9 percent for a non-UK or
  business card, plus 0.4 to 0.7 percent conversion, with free payout. This is
  the cheapest option because the `wise` instrument kind already exists.
  **Unverified and blocking: whether this is live for a Mexico-registered Wise
  Business account.** It is Business-only, which a teacher trading as an
  individual may not have.
- **A Mercado Pago "link de pago"** as a new instrument kind for Mexican
  teachers — card, debit, SPEI, OXXO, and meses sin intereses, at 3.49 percent
  plus 4 MXN for instant settlement, accepting Visa, Mastercard and American
  Express including foreign-issued cards. Instalments alone are a large
  conversion lever in Mexico. The link is a URL, so it is machine-checkable and
  does not breach D-124's rule against a free-form payee field.
- **PayPal.** The most expensive, and the only one that answers the stated
  objection head-on: buyer protection is the thing a nervous student already
  recognises.

**None of these gives the platform a fee hook.** That is the honest trade, and
it costs nothing today, because the rail takes no cut in the first place
([D-143](./D-143.md)). The day the platform wants a take rate outside the
circle, it runs straight back into this document.

## What this does NOT reverse

- **Stripe Connect stays**, and the one lifetime Stripe checkout is not
  evidence against it. There are no Connect-circle teachers yet; the rail is
  built, costs nothing idle, and is the only one that pushes compliance onto
  Stripe. D-58's circle is unchanged and must not be widened.
- **This is not a reversal of [D-01](./D-01.md) or [D-02](./D-02.md).** D-01
  moved the PLATFORM's processor from Mercado Pago to Stripe; a Mercado Pago
  link held by the teacher is not the platform's processor and creates none of
  the OAuth and token-refresh burden D-01 walked away from. D-02 removed OXXO
  because asynchronous cash settlement meant a student could not be confirmed
  into the calendar until the money landed — an objection the manual rail
  **already absorbs**, because self-attestation followed by teacher
  confirmation is asynchronous by construction. Absorbed is not free: see the
  risks.

## What would reopen this

Any one of: a teacher inside the Connect circle signing up, which makes the
card rail real without any of this; a measured drop-off on the manual rail once
there are enough real students to measure one; or a teacher whose country has
no workable teacher-held card instrument at all. Volume that makes the
regulatory question proportionate to answer would also reopen it — but that is a
decision with an accountant and a solicitor in the room, not a
payments-integration decision.

## What was affected

Nothing shipped. Two follow-ups, which are tracked outside this repository per
[D-110](./D-110.md):

- **`payment_received` carries no `method` property**, so a confirmed payment
  cannot be attributed back to the rail that produced it. `checkout_started`
  and `checkout_submitted` both carry it; this is the one gap that stops the
  funnel composing. Small change, and it is what makes the question in this doc
  answerable later.
- **Instrument-kind candidates** above, in the order listed, gated on the Wise
  Business verification.

## Unresolved risks

- **The Wise question is unverified and it decides the cheapest option.**
  Whether a Mexico-registered Wise Business account can issue card-accepting
  payment links is a support-ticket answer, not a documentation answer. Nothing
  should be built against it until someone asks.
- **A teacher-held card link may not fully solve trust.** The buyer protection
  belongs to the teacher's provider, not to AgendaProfe, and a dispute or
  chargeback lands on the teacher — who has already taught the class. That is a
  real cost transferred to her, and nobody has asked her about it.
- **The premise cuts both ways.** "There is no evidence of a conversion
  problem" is also "there is no evidence any fix works". At this volume the
  first version of whatever ships will be chosen on judgement.
- **Reintroducing OXXO through a Mercado Pago link re-imports D-02's
  asynchrony.** The manual rail absorbs it, but a student who has several days
  to walk to a shop is a student whose booking sits unconfirmed for several
  days, and the cancellation and slot-holding logic has never been exercised
  against that.
- **Fee levels are quoted from vendor marketing pages** as of 2026-08-28 and
  are the kind of number that drifts. Re-check before any of it reaches a
  pricing surface.
