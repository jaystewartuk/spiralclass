# D-161. The heavy tier leaves the laptop — a second set of Linux visual baselines is what makes it possible

**What changed.** `.github/workflows/heavy.yml` runs the mutation spot-check,
the integration suite and the browser suites on `ubuntu-latest`, as three
parallel jobs, on every pull request into `main`. The registry grows a third
tier, `heavy`, so the workflow can ask for those steps without dragging ten
minutes of `test-web` in front of them. `apps/web/tests/visual` gains 120
`-linux.png` baselines beside the 120 `-darwin.png` ones it already had.

This is the piece of work [D-157](./D-157.md) named and deferred, in its own
words: _"That is the single piece of work that would move integration and E2E
onto runners, and it is worth doing."_

## Why now, and why the premise for deferring it had already expired

D-157 did not defer this on cost or on principle. It deferred it on one
concrete technical fact:

> The visual-regression baselines under `apps/web/tests/visual` are
> `-darwin.png`, so `scripts/ci/e2e.sh` cannot go green on a Linux runner until
> they are regenerated for Linux or the sweep is made platform-neutral.

That is true and it was the whole blocker. It was also, on inspection, not a
blocker that needed the baselines REPLACED — which is what "regenerated for
Linux" implies, and which would have cost the laptop its own suite. Playwright
suffixes a snapshot with `process.platform`. Two sets can coexist, each
authoritative for the machine that reads it, and neither one degrading the
other. So the fix is additive: keep darwin, add linux, and both runners assert
against images made where they run.

The alternative D-157 also offered — making the sweep platform-neutral — was
rejected. Platform-neutral in practice means widening `maxDiffPixelRatio` until
Linux and macOS font rasterisation both fit inside it. The suite already
tolerates `0.01` and the header of `scripts/ci/e2e.sh` records what that
tolerance costs: two lines of grey text swapping places on a 1280x2184 page is
about `0.003`, which PASSES. Widening the threshold to cover a whole different
font stack would not make the guard portable; it would make it decorative.

## What this actually buys, which is not free minutes

The minutes were already free — [D-157](./D-157.md) settled that. Three things
are bought, in descending order of how much they matter:

- **The laptop stops being held for 20-40 minutes at a time.** The browser
  suite takes the machine lock (D-146) for its whole duration: one shared
  Postgres container, one port 3000, two production builds at a 6GB heap
  ceiling and a fleet of Chromium workers. Several Claude Code sessions work
  this repo at once, each in its own worktree, and every one of their pushes
  queues behind that. This was the single largest source of waiting in the
  loop, and it is the thing the operator asked to remove.
- **The heavy suites now run BEFORE a merge rather than after one.** Under
  [D-146](./D-146.md) they ran once per batch, against `main` with every merge
  already in it — so the first thing a broken combination did was land. That
  batching was not a preference; it was arithmetic against one laptop that
  could only run the suites serially. A runner is concurrent and there are
  three of them, so the reason expired with the constraint.
- **Three jobs instead of one.** The legs are independent and `fail-fast` is
  off, so a red mutation spot-check no longer throws away the browser answer,
  which is the one that takes longest to get and is hardest to reproduce by
  hand.

## The two rules this had to obey, and how

⚠️ **`scripts/ci/steps.mjs` stays the single source of truth for what "green"
means.** That is the condition D-157 re-admitted Actions on, and a workflow
that runs steps as SEPARATE JOBS strains it harder than `gate.yml` ever did:
the obvious way to write three parallel legs is to name three legs in YAML.

Such a list is correct the day it is written and silently wrong the first time
a heavy step is added — the new step runs in `pnpm gate:full` on the laptop,
runs in no job on a runner, and nothing anywhere says so. So the matrix is
**derived**. A `plan` job runs `gate.mjs --tier heavy --list --json`, and the
`heavy` job fans out over the answer. A step added to the registry gets a
runner in the same commit.

The guard in `apps/web/tests/config/local-gate.test.ts` asserts this directly:
`heavy.yml` must contain the `--list --json` call and the `fromJSON`, and must
not name any heavy step id as a literal in anything it executes. Comments are
stripped before that check, deliberately — the file's header names `e2e.sh`
while explaining why no job may invoke it, and a guard that cannot tell an
instruction from its own prohibition is one people delete.

**The tier had to be nameable without the fast half.** Before this there was no
way to ask for the expensive suites alone, because there was no reason to want
one: the laptop ran both halves and `full` meaning "everything" was exactly
right. A workflow asking for `full` would re-run `test-web` — measured at
**9m53s** on a runner against **64.7s** on the laptop — to reach three steps
that were already the point. So a step now declares which HALF it is in
(`fast` or `heavy`), and a TIER is a selection over halves.

⚠️ **`full` is still every step and still what `pnpm promote` runs.** That is
the one meaning that must not narrow. It certifies the exact commit that ships,
and a promote that skipped the fast half because "a runner did it" would be
trusting a green from another machine and another moment.

## The standing cost, which is a bookkeeping cost

Two baseline sets means a change to what a page LOOKS LIKE needs both
regenerated, and the regeneration command can only ever see one of them.
`VISUAL_UPDATE_SNAPSHOTS=all bash scripts/ci/e2e.sh` rewrites the set belonging
to the machine it runs on. So:

- Restyle a page, regenerate on the laptop, review the diffs, merge — and the
  runner goes red on exactly the routes just fixed.
- Add a route, get a darwin baseline locally — and the runner finds no linux
  baseline, **writes one from whatever rendered that day, and reports green.**

The second is the dangerous one, because it is silent and because it looks like
coverage. Playwright's default is `updateSnapshots: 'missing'`; a capture with
one baseline is not half-covered, it is uncovered.

`apps/web/tests/visual/manifest.test.ts` holds the invariant: the two sets must
name exactly the same captures, and both must be non-empty. It runs in the
**fast** tier, so it fails on the laptop and on `gate.yml` within seconds —
before the heavy tier has even started, and long before anything reaches
`main`. A third assertion checks the sets against `regression.spec.ts`'s own
route selection, because two equally stale sets agree with each other perfectly.

Regenerating the Linux half is a documented dispatch, and it has to run on the
runner rather than under emulation:

```
gh workflow run heavy.yml -f update_visual_baselines=all --ref <branch>
gh run download <id> -n visual-baselines-linux \
  -D apps/web/tests/visual/regression.spec.ts-snapshots/
```

arm64 Linux in a container rasterises text differently from the amd64 runner,
so baselines made that way would be wrong in the worst available shape: they
pass on the machine that made them and fail in CI.

## What is deliberately NOT done

- **`scripts/ship-pr.sh` still runs the full tier on the laptop against merged
  `main`, and this record does not change that.** It is tempting — it is the
  last routine 40-minute laptop hold — but it does two jobs and only one of
  them is now redundant. Finding breakage is covered: every PR ran the heavy
  tier before merging. **Writing the receipt is not.** `pnpm promote` reuses a
  green full-tier receipt bound to the exact commit, and a runner's filesystem
  is thrown away, so deleting the local run would push those 40 minutes into
  the promote instead of removing them. Changing that means re-deciding what
  certifies a release, which is [D-120](./D-120.md) and D-157 territory and is
  its own decision.
- **No `schedule:` trigger.** D-157's refusal stands and this is not the
  occasion to revisit it.
- **No larger runner.** `ubuntu-latest` and nothing else. Larger runners are
  billed by the minute even on a public repository, and the guard that asserts
  this is untouched.
- **The a11y and E2E suites are not split out of the browser leg.** They share
  the expensive part — one seeded database and one production build — and
  `scripts/ci/e2e.sh` already reports the three verdicts separately. Splitting
  them would buy parallelism by paying for two more `next build`s.

## Unresolved risks

- **The first run of this workflow asserts nothing about the visual sweep.**
  With no `-linux.png` committed, Playwright writes all 120 and the leg may go
  green on a retry having compared each image against itself. The fast-tier
  parity guard is what stops that reaching `main`, and it is the only thing
  that does. **Look at the images before committing them.**
- **`heavy.yml` runs on every PR, so a PR is now ~30-45 minutes of runner time
  rather than ~10.** Free, and concurrent with the fast gate rather than after
  it, so it does not delay a merge that only waits on `local-gate`. It does
  mean the "everything is green" moment arrives later than it used to.
- **A fork PR gets no turbo cache and no secrets**, so the heavy tier runs cold
  there. Correct, and slower — the same trade D-157 recorded for `gate.yml`.
- **Two baseline sets will drift out of parity at some point**, and the guard
  catches it as a red fast tier with a message naming the regeneration command.
  That is a good failure, but it is a failure that will happen, and it happens
  at the moment someone is trying to land a design change.
- **The `-linux.png` set doubles the repository's baseline image weight.** 120
  full-page PNGs at six viewports; worth watching if the sweep ever grows to
  the ~130 routes its own header anticipates.
