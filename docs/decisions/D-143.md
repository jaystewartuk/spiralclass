# D-143. The teacher becomes the merchant of record: Stripe Connect direct charges

**What changed.** The platform stops being the merchant of record for lesson
payments. Under separate charges and transfers a student's money landed on the
platform's own Stripe balance and a Transfer forwarded the settled net to the
teacher's connected account. It does not any more: the charge is created **on
her connected account**, settles in her country, and Stripe pays her out
locally. The platform never holds, withholds or forwards a penny of it.

Concretely, connected accounts move to the **Accounts v2 API**
(`/v2/core/accounts`) with the configuration Stripe's own guidance gives for a
SaaS platform whose sellers own their customer relationship:

- **`dashboard: "full"`** — she gets a real Stripe account and dashboard, and
  Stripe handles her verification, compliance updates and support.
- **`merchant` configuration** — she accepts payments directly. Direct charges
  are the only charge type a full dashboard supports; `full` paired with
  destination or separate charges is a blocked combination, so this is not a
  halfway house that exists.
- **`customer` configuration on the same `Account`** — the platform bills her
  for software against that same object, retiring the `Account` to `Customer`
  mapping a v1 integration is forced to maintain forever.
- **`fees_collector: "stripe"`, `losses_collector: "stripe"`** — she pays
  Stripe's fees at her own country's rates, and a chargeback debits her balance
  rather than the platform's.

**Why it likely changed.** Three problems shared one cause, and only one fix
addressed all three.

- **Coverage.** The old gate was Stripe's cross-border _payout_ circle, because
  a Transfer had to cross a border Stripe would not cross. That capped the card
  rail at 31 countries and excluded Mexico, Brazil, Japan, Singapore, Thailand,
  Australia — a platform whose teachers and students are explicitly anywhere in
  the world could not take a card from most of them.
- **Conversion.** Which payment methods a checkout can offer is decided by the
  **merchant's** country. A UK platform charge cannot offer OXXO or SPEI to a
  Mexican buyer, Boleto to a Brazilian one, iDEAL to a Dutch one or BLIK to a
  Polish one, at any price. Moving merchant of record to the teacher is the only
  route to those, not an optimisation of an existing one. (This bullet said
  "Pix to a Brazilian one" until 2026-08-31; Pix turns out to be unreachable on
  Accounts v2 — see the addendum.)
- **Liability and structure.** Disputes debited the platform first, to be
  clawed back from a teacher who might already have withdrawn.

This is the mechanism [D-137](./D-137.md) was reaching for and did not find.
D-137 promoted "the platform never touches the funds" from an incidental
property of [D-113](./D-113.md) to a constraint, on grounds it set out at the
time, and then went looking for a card rail outside the Connect circle
in **teacher-held third-party links** — a Wise payment link, a Mercado Pago link
de pago, PayPal. It explicitly left Connect alone: _"Stripe Connect stays and
D-58's circle is unchanged."_ That last clause is what this reverses. The
constraint is unchanged and now satisfied more completely than the
third-party-link plan would have managed, because a Connect direct charge makes
the teacher merchant of record on Stripe itself — no second processor, no second
dispute model, no per-teacher link to verify.

It also retires the reason [D-58](./D-58.md) excluded Mexico. D-58 was right:
a UK entity genuinely cannot Transfer to a Mexican connected account. The error
was never the circle; it was choosing a funds flow that needed one.

**The country list is measured, not inferred.** Every code in
`SUPPORTED_CONNECT_COUNTRIES` was probed against Stripe test mode on
2026-08-30, with the exact configuration the application creates, before any of
this was written. 44 countries accept it. Four refuse: **IN** returns
"card_payments capability is not supported for this account type and this
account's country", and **ZA**, **NG** and **ID** return "v2 Account creation
with configuration.merchant is currently unavailable for your platform".

**IS is a genuine narrowing.** Iceland was inside the old circle and Stripe will
not create a merchant account for it. It is the one country this takes off the
card rail, against thirteen it adds, and it is called out in the constant and
its test so nobody restores it on the assumption that EEA membership implies
support. Bulgaria is in but must not be created with BGN, which Stripe now
refuses in favour of EUR.

**No cut may be taken at the charge.** The 8 percent and 3 percent marketplace
commission is removed outright. It was withheld from the settled net before the
payout, which only worked because the money passed through the platform balance.
Collecting it now would mean a Stripe application fee, and cross-border
application-fee support is undocumented, account-specific and known to fail for
exactly the markets being opened. A take-rate remains available as a pricing
decision — meter GMV from the webhook and bill it on her own subscription
invoice, which works in every country — but it is a billing feature and must
never become a payments one. `subscriptions-config.ts` says so where the
constants used to be, so the application-fee approach is not re-derived.

**What was affected.**

- **Deleted:** `payments/transfer.ts`, its Inngest job, and
  `createTransfer` / `reverseTransfer` / `getTransfer` across the client, stub
  and schemas. The refund clawback on all four surfaces and both webhook paths
  went with them — a refund now debits her own balance, so there is nothing to
  reverse. The `payments` table drops `stripe_transfer_id`,
  `commission_centavos` and `teacher_net_centavos`.
- **The Stripe-coupled billing layer was deleted and rebuilt**, on the
  operator's explicit instruction, rather than refactored in place. What
  survived the deletion was everything that never called Stripe: `lifecycle.ts`
  is a pure database state machine, `commission.ts` is the unrelated Ambassador
  referral payout, and `sweep`, `enforce`, `service` and `entitlements` feed
  more than twenty feature gates across the product.

  The rebuild bills the teacher's own `Account` via `customer_account` — no
  `Customer` object for anyone, and no mapping table. A teacher Stripe refuses
  as a merchant still gets a `customer`-configuration-only Account, so being
  billable never depends on being a merchant. The Billing Portal accepts an
  Account too, so card updates and cancellation work unchanged.

- **`reconcile-paid.ts` changed shape rather than shrinking.** Its Stripe arm
  keyed on `stripeTransferId IS NULL`, meaning "the payout never ran". There is
  no payout. Both rails now share the signal the manual rail already used — an
  unconsumed intended slot — so the two-arm `OR` collapses to one query while
  the hole it guards is unchanged.
- **The currency rail-split is gone.** Card-rail teachers were offered only GBP,
  USD and EUR, correct while their money landed on a GBP balance and wrong now
  that a Mexican teacher's own account settles in MXN. Both rails offer the same
  curated list, and an explicit currency pick survives any country change.
- **`pricing-fees.ts` lost both its premises.** It was Stripe UK's table,
  defensible only because card-rail teachers priced in 2-decimal currencies. She
  now pays her own country's rate and may price in a 0-decimal one, where the
  hardcoded 100 minor units was a 100x error rather than a rounding one. Both
  helpers take a currency and scale by `currencyExponent`.
- **The manual transfer rail ([D-124](./D-124.md)) is untouched** and remains
  the fallback for every country Stripe refuses.

**Unresolved risks.**

- **The API version pin moves from `2024-06-20` to `2026-06-24.dahlia`** — a
  two-year jump across the whole integration, and the largest single source of
  regression risk here. Everything Stripe-shaped should be re-read against the
  changelog rather than assumed.

  **This is NOT a preview API, correcting an earlier reading in this very
  document's drafting.** The exploration used `2026-08-26.preview` because
  that is the version Stripe's own Accounts v2 documentation shows, and the
  risk was written up as "building the money path on a contract that can still
  move". It was then measured: the identical account — `dashboard: "full"`,
  `merchant` + `customer`, `fees_collector` and `losses_collector` both
  `"stripe"` — creates cleanly on `2026-06-24.dahlia`, which is the **stable**
  version bundled with the pinned `stripe` SDK (22.3.2). No preview version is
  used anywhere. Pin the SDK's own bundled version and do not reintroduce a
  `.preview` pin without a reason that survives a probe.

- **The charge itself is unverified end to end.** Account creation, the
  configuration and the country list are measured; that the local methods
  actually _surface at checkout_, and that Adaptive Pricing engages on a
  connected-account charge, are not — a freshly created test account is
  `restricted / requirements_past_due` and needs onboarding first. This is the
  first thing the build half must prove. (Both are now settled — see the
  2026-08-31 addendum. Completing a payment is not.)
- **No lesson payment has ever been processed on this rail**, in test or in
  production. Subscription billing is rebuilt and covered by tests, but the
  lesson path's proof still requires an onboarded teacher, and a card has still
  not been charged. What the 2026-08-31 addendum below settles is the narrower
  claim that OXXO and SPEI can surface at all.
- **Saved cards and SCA.** Where her country enforces Strong Customer
  Authentication and the platform's does not, setup for future payments may not
  trigger it. The documented workaround is `on_behalf_of`, which Stripe's own
  Connect guidance puts out of scope and redirects to sales — so this needs
  solving inside direct charges, and is not yet.
- **The operator's tax and regulatory position is not recorded here** — see
  [D-159](./D-159.md) for why this repository does not carry it.

## Addendum, 2026-08-31 — the capability is only half the gate

An early teacher onboarded onto this rail (Mexico), and a live checkout
was opened against her account. Three things came out of it, in rising order of
how wrong the original write-up was.

### 1. OXXO and SPEI surface — but a capability alone does not do it

The open risk above was whether the local methods actually reach a buyer. They
do, and her account now offers them. The route there is the part worth writing
down, because the obvious check gives a false positive.

Her account reported `card_payments: active`, `oxxo_payments: active`,
`mx_bank_transfer_payments: active`, charges and payouts enabled, no
requirements due. On that evidence the claim looked proven. It was not: the
Checkout Session created on her account came back
`payment_method_types: ["card"]`, and the hosted page rendered cards only.

What actually decides what Checkout offers is the connected account's **payment
method configuration**, which is created as a CHILD of the platform's parent
configuration. Hers inherited `oxxo: {available: false, value: off}` and
`customer_balance: {available: false, value: off}` from a **UK** parent — the
give-away being that she had also inherited Bancontact, EPS and iDEAL as
on-but-unavailable. Her Mexican capabilities were live and irrelevant.

The parent cannot be fixed from code. `GET
/v1/payment_method_configurations/<parent>` answers _"Platform parent
configurations can only be managed via the dashboard."_ It is Settings →
Connect → Payment methods → the connected-accounts configuration, where **OXXO**
(under Vouchers) and **Bank Transfers** — which is how SPEI surfaces — were both
"Off by default". Switching both to "On by default" propagated to her inherited
config immediately: `oxxo` and `customer_balance` both now read
`available: true, value: on`.

**So a new local payment method needs two things, and the API-side one is the
one that lies to you.** Ask for the capability at account creation, and switch
the method on in that Dashboard parent configuration. An active capability with
the parent switched off is silently card-only, on an account that looks
perfectly healthy.

### 2. Pix is unreachable on Accounts v2, and `pix_payments` was breaking Brazil

The same Dashboard page labels Pix, PayPal, Alipay, WeChat Pay, Bizum, UPI and
PayTo **"Only supported in v1 accounts"**. D-143 chose v2 deliberately and for
good reasons, so the cost of that choice includes Pix — which is not a minor
line item, being instant and the majority of Brazilian ecommerce, with Boleto
as the slow fallback.

Probing the API was worse than the Dashboard note suggested. `pix_payments` is
not a v2 field at all:

```
configuration.merchant.capabilities.pix_payments:
  Unknown field, did you mean fpx_payments, p24_payments, zip_payments?
```

v2 **400s on an unknown capability name** rather than ignoring it, so
`pix_payments` in the registry was not a Brazilian teacher missing Pix — it was
a Brazilian teacher unable to create a connected account at all, failing on the
Stripe onboarding card with nothing to tell her why. Probing the rest of the
registry found a second one: `fpx_payments` (Malaysia) is a real v2 capability
that refuses to be requested without `business_type`, which account creation
does not collect. Same hard failure.

Both are removed. Brazil keeps `boleto_payments`; Malaysia has no entry and gets
cards. `V2_REJECTED_CAPABILITIES` in `packages/shared/src/stripe-capabilities.ts`
records both with their measured errors, and a test fails if either returns.

**The generalisation, which is the actual lesson:** the v1 and v2 capability
vocabularies are different, and the registry had been written from v1 knowledge.
No unit test could catch it, because a mocked Stripe accepts any string — the
name is only meaningful to the real API. `scripts/probe-v2-capabilities.mjs`
makes measuring a command, and is a prerequisite for adding a country, the same
way `SUPPORTED_CONNECT_COUNTRIES` is measured rather than reasoned about.

### 3. What is still unproven

**No card has been charged.** A live Checkout Session was created on her account
and the hosted page rendered — branded with her name, with Adaptive Pricing
offering the buyer US$23.00 or MX$380.00 for the same MX$380 package — but the
payment was never completed. Settlement into her Mexican balance, the
`payment_intent.succeeded` webhook, the asynchronous OXXO/SPEI confirmation
path, and refund behaviour on her balance are all still unverified.

## Addendum, 2026-08-31 — a teacher's existing Stripe account can never be used

Every teacher who already has a Stripe account will try to connect it, and
cannot. **Accounts v2 has no OAuth**, so there is no mechanism by which a
platform adopts an account it did not create — the v1 "Connect your existing
Stripe account" flow has no v2 equivalent, and this is a property of the API
choice, not a missing feature to build.

An early teacher hit it immediately, with `acct_1ExampleTeacher0`,
created before she ever saw SpiralClass, and it stays permanently invisible to
the platform:

```
account_invalid: The provided key does not have access to account
'acct_1ExampleTeacher0' (or that account does not exist).
```

She onboarded a platform-created account (`acct_1ExamplePlatform`) instead,
which is the only route. So:

- **She will end up with two Stripe accounts and two dashboards**, and the one
  she already knows how to log into is the wrong one. Expect her to look there
  for her lesson payments, find nothing, and report it as a bug. That is the
  support cost of this design and it is unavoidable — worth saying in onboarding
  copy rather than discovering per teacher.
- **The account SpiralClass creates is the only one that matters.** Do not add a
  "paste your Stripe account id" field, or an id-matching heuristic: the platform
  has no access to an account it did not create, so any such input can only
  produce `account_invalid` at the first charge.
- **Reversing this means reversing v2**, which would also mean giving up the
  `customer` configuration that removes the Account-to-Customer mapping table.
  Weigh it as that trade, not as an onboarding tweak.

## Addendum, 2026-08-31 — every account this platform creates is PERMANENT

**A connected account created under this decision's configuration can never be
deleted or closed by the platform.** Not by `DELETE /v1/accounts/{id}`, not by
`POST /v2/core/accounts/{id}/close`, not with the platform's own live secret
key. Stripe refuses both:

```
stripe_loss_liable_cannot_be_deleted: This method may not be used on livemode
accounts where `controller[losses][payments]=stripe and dashboard=full`,
which includes Standard accounts.
```

That is not a permissions gap to widen. It is a verbatim description of what
D-143 chose, confirmed on a real account:

```json
"controller": {
  "losses": { "payments": "stripe" },
  "stripe_dashboard": { "type": "full" },
  "requirement_collection": "stripe",
  "type": "application"
}
```

The reasoning is Stripe's and it is sound: the platform is not liable for these
accounts' losses and does not collect their requirements, so it does not get to
destroy them either. The account belongs to the teacher. Keeping
`losses_collector: "stripe"` is the whole point of the decision — it is what
keeps a chargeback off the platform's balance and the platform out of
client-money territory — so this consequence is not negotiable while D-143
stands.

### What that cost, concretely

A CSP header missing `connect-js.stripe.com` made the embedded onboarding card
render empty, the component retried, and every retry created a new connected
account because the idempotency key was a fresh UUID per call. That produced
**24 orphan live accounts** for one teacher in about ninety minutes. Both ends
are fixed — a stable `connect-account-${teacher.id}` key, and the CSP host —
but the 24 accounts are permanent. They are inert (`charges_enabled: false`,
`details_submitted: false`, unreferenced by any row) and they will sit in the
Connect list forever.

### The rule this implies

**Account creation has no undo, so treat it like a migration, not a retry.**

- The idempotency key on `createConnectedAccount` is not an optimisation and
  must never become optional or random. It is the only thing standing between a
  UI bug and permanent damage.
- The guarded persist in `stripe-connect.ts` — `updateMany` on
  `stripeAccountId: null`, adopting the winner's id if the claim loses — is the
  second half of that and belongs with it.
- Anything that can call account creation in a loop (a retrying component, a
  polling effect, a webhook handler) needs the same scrutiny as a destructive
  operation, because it is one.

`POST /v1/accounts/{id}/reject` is the only remaining lever and is UNTESTED
here; it would mark them rejected rather than remove them, and it is not known
whether it is permitted under this same controller configuration.
