# D-17. Lesson content is native Markdown the platform's own model drafts from inputs the teacher owns; the platform writes no curriculum

The canonical form of a class's or library item's content is a Markdown body on
`LibraryMaterial` that the app renders, not a PDF. AI drafting runs through the
platform's Claude key ([`lib/ai/anthropic.ts`](../../apps/web/src/lib/ai/anthropic.ts)),
is Pro-gated and capped at `CLASS_CONTENT_AI_MONTHLY_CAP` (100 a month,
[`lib/materials/config.ts`](../../apps/web/src/lib/materials/config.ts)), and is
seeded only from data the teacher owns — her student's profile, her focus tags,
her lesson templates and her style settings — never from a curriculum the
platform authors.

Behaviour lives in [classes: lesson content](../features/classes-lesson-content.md)
and [library materials](../features/library-materials.md).

## Why

Teachers were writing lessons in a separate ChatGPT tab, retyping the same
context every class, and attaching PDFs that cannot reflow on a phone. The
platform already holds what an outside chatbot cannot: the student's level,
what her notebook marks as covered, and the teacher's notes on the student's
interests and goals. That context is the advantage; canned content is not.

## Options considered

- **PDF as the stored form** — fixed pages, unreadable on a phone. Kept as an
  input (a teacher may still attach one) and an export (the print view), never
  the source of truth.
- **The teacher's own external chatbot account** — per-teacher lock-in,
  inconsistent results, and no access to student context.
- **A platform curriculum per subject** — a content-publishing business, and the
  lock-in the teacher-owned level ladder exists to avoid. In July 2026 the first
  teacher proposed her full method as the content backbone; her method stayed
  hers, and only its _structure_ entered the product, as her own template.
- **A fully editable system prompt** — a teacher could break the rules that keep
  the lesson in the language she teaches and about the right student. She gets
  presets (tone, learner age, language variety) and an appended note instead.
- **Vocabulary tied to the CEFR level** — C1 material pulled in words the
  teachers themselves did not know.
- **A one-to-one / group toggle** — a control with one possible value.
- **A sticky default-template column** — needed a migration; an explicit pick
  that defaults to her first template did not.

## What it cost

- No automatic PDF-to-native conversion; moving existing PDFs over is gradual.
- Every prompt rule is an instruction, not a filter. Tests assert each
  directive is present, never that the model obeys it, and there is no eval set;
  a regression arrives as a teacher's report.
- One template skeleton applies at every level; a teacher who wants different
  structures per level keeps several templates.
- The vocabulary default changed output for every teacher: unset now means an
  explicit `everyday` directive, not the level's old pull toward rare words.
- Focus-tag labels and style directives are not translated.

## What it constrains

- **No HTML sink.** Stored content is shown to other people, so the material
  parser ([`material-doc/parse.ts`](../../packages/shared/src/material-doc/parse.ts))
  knows only Markdown constructs and renders no raw HTML. Never add
  `rehype-raw` or an HTML sink to a material renderer; see
  [material visuals](../architecture/material-visuals.md).
- **Platform key only.** `hasAnthropicCreds()` false hides AI and leaves
  hand-authoring working. Generation never saves: generate, review, then an
  explicit save. Model choice is [D-87](./D-87.md).
- **Authoring is Pro, viewing is free.** `gateProFeature(…, "class_content")`;
  on downgrade content is read-only, never deleted.
- **No platform curriculum.** Do not seed lesson templates the way focus tags
  are seeded. Focus-tag packs are data chosen by the language she teaches
  ([`lib/focus-packs.ts`](../../apps/web/src/lib/focus-packs.ts)), seeding is
  additive, and she can rename, add or archive every tag.
- **Templates are optional and tenant-scoped.** No template equals the generic
  shape; a foreign or unknown id is silently "no template", pinned in
  `tests/materials/resolve-template.test.ts` ("returns null for a foreign /
  unknown id"); the injected body is bounded.
- **Teacher settings defer to the platform rules.** Style directives and the
  free-text note come after the language and student-name rules and are worded
  to defer to them — `tests/materials/prompt.test.ts`, "keeps custom
  instructions subordinate to the language and name rules".
- **Vocabulary is its own axis**, always emitted, resolved class override →
  teacher default → `everyday` by `resolveVocabulary()` in
  `packages/shared`.
- **One-to-one is always stated.** `lessonFormatDirective()` in
  [`lib/materials/prompt.ts`](../../apps/web/src/lib/materials/prompt.ts) is
  emitted by the material, refine and podcast-script builders, early and ahead
  of the teacher's note. `LESSON_FORMATS` has one value; a group format is a new
  value plus a case, and needs a booking with more than one student first.
- **The student profile is the teacher's.** `interests` and `goals` are in her
  data export, never the student's.

## How we got here

- **2026-06-23.** Native content per booking (a `class_content` table), Markdown
  rendered with raw HTML off, AI compose on `claude-opus-4-8` via the platform
  key, Pro-gated, then capped at 100 generations a month.
- **2026-06-24.** Four input layers: student profile, focus tags, a soft
  `discipline` code choosing a seed pack (Spanish first; English, music, maths
  and a generic pack beside it), and reusable library bodies.
- **2026-07-03.** Focus tags became teacher-editable.
- **2026-07-08.** Her lesson template drives compose; no migration.
- **2026-07-15.** `discipline` gave way to `teachers.target_language` and the
  non-language packs were removed ([D-72](./D-72.md)).
- **2026-07-19.** The compose model moved to Sonnet 5 ([D-87](./D-87.md)); the
  same month added style settings, after a teacher found the output too
  academic, and a separate vocabulary axis.
- **July 2026, undated.** A teacher reported group and pair work in generated
  lessons; the one-to-one directive followed.
- **Later.** The per-booking content table and class attachments merged into
  `LibraryMaterial`, so one prompt builder and one cap serve class content,
  library items, "Edit with AI" refine and podcast scripts.
- **Open.** Packages for two people who take every class together exist since
  2026-09-27; the directive still says "exactly one student" and has not been
  reconciled with them.
