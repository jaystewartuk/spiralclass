# D-72. Languages are codes from two registries, held in four columns that never stand in for each other, and each public URL renders one language

A teacher's subject is a BCP-47 code in `teachers.target_language`, drawn from
the one language registry in `packages/shared/src/languages.ts`; the languages
the interface is written in are rows in
`packages/shared/src/i18n/locale-registry.ts`; and `target_language`,
`teaching_language`, `locale` and `booking_page_locale` are four separate facts,
none derived from another. A public marketing, help or legal page takes its
language from its URL (bare is English, `/es/…`, `/fr/…`); the booking funnel
`/b/**` takes it from the teacher's `booking_page_locale` through
`publicFunnelLocaleFor()`, never from the visitor.

The audience is independent teachers of **any subject**, and the public copy
says so. The schema is narrower than the copy: the column still holds a language
code. Both halves are true at once.

## Why

Every language fact here has been inferred from another one at least once, and
each inference was right for the first teacher by coincidence and wrong as
soon as two facts diverged. The first teacher reads Spanish, teaches Spanish,
and sells to English speakers. That is three of the four columns, and no rule
recovers any one of them from the others.

The funnel has a second constraint. Its social card is rendered with no visitor
present, so it can only follow a value stored on the teacher. A per-visitor rule
puts a card in one language over a page in another. Half of every booking page
is also the teacher's own words (headline, bio, package names), each one string
in one language, so translating the platform's half per visitor produces a page
in two languages.

Public pages need one URL per language because search engines index one
language per URL. Google's crawler sends no `Accept-Language`, so pages that
negotiated by header were most likely indexed in English only. That is inferred
from Google's published crawler behaviour, not read from Search Console.

## Options considered

- **Free text, or a subject registry, for `target_language`** — a migration, a
  registry, a picker and a prompt rewrite, with no teacher waiting on it, and
  much harder to reverse than copy. A stored code gives derived labels and one
  list, with `asr` and `gen` as capability flags rather than second lists.
- **Narrow the copy to "any language"** — reads a music or maths teacher out
  of a booking, payment and reminder product that fits her exactly.
- **All ~7,900 ISO 639-3 codes** — about 95% have no Spanish name in CLDR and
  essentially none are taught. A wrong code silently mislabels a teacher's
  language, which is worse than an absent one, so additions are curated rows.
- **Subdomains, `/en/` on English too, header negotiation plus `hreflang`, or
  redirect by IP** — respectively: a certificate and a cookie spanning
  subdomains; a redirect hop on every English link already shared; `hreflang`
  needs distinct URLs; and an IP overrides the reader's own stated language.
- **A visitor language switcher, or machine-translating her words, on `/b/**`**
  — the split-language funnel, or copy in her name she never read.
- **A full catalog per regional locale (`es-ES` beside `es`)** — thousands of
  near-identical keys kept in step by hand, whose differences drift by accident.
- **Each legal translation controls in its own language**, or **machine
  translation "for convenience"** — the operator would carry N contracts no one
  can read, and a mistranslation becomes binding.

## What it cost

- A non-language teacher meets a language-shaped product on day one.
  Onboarding requires a teaching language (`timezone-form.tsx` blocks submit; the
  server action enforces it), the picker offers only languages, every
  `focusTagSeedFor()` branch seeds a language pack
  ([`focus-packs.ts`](../../apps/web/src/lib/focus-packs.ts)), and
  [`materials/prompt.ts`](../../apps/web/src/lib/materials/prompt.ts) states the
  code as the lesson's subject. No such teacher is live, so nothing measures it.
- A teacher whose buyers read two languages must choose one.
- A reader in Spain reads Latin American Spanish. The register is written down
  only in [`glossary.ts`](../../packages/shared/src/i18n/glossary.ts).
- Each launched language costs two person-made legal translations, and each
  material change to the English terms costs an update to every one.
- Google discourages redirecting by language. Crawlers are never redirected and
  `x-default` names the bare URL, which mitigates but does not remove the risk.

## What it constrains

- **Never derive one language column from another.** The table and the funnel
  rule are in [`docs/development/i18n.md`](../development/i18n.md).
- **Public copy must not rescope the audience to language teaching.**
  [`i18n.test.ts`](../../packages/shared/src/i18n/i18n.test.ts) ("the marketing
  copy sells to teachers of any subject") fails on it. Nor is the copy licence to
  treat `target_language` as free text: widening it is a separate decision. The
  funnel, payment rails and reminders stay subject-neutral, because the promise
  rests on them.
- **`target_language` is asked at onboarding, before the first focus-picker
  render.** `getTeacherFocusTags()` self-seeds on an empty read and seeding is
  additive, so a null seeds the neutral pack permanently and setting the
  language later only appends. Every write path seeds explicitly.
- **A public page's language comes from its URL and nothing else.** The
  allowlist in [`localized-paths.ts`](../../packages/shared/src/i18n/localized-paths.ts)
  is the only way a path gains a prefix; `/b/**` is never prefixed and reads no
  visitor language. A link inside the site is never redirected. A page is
  advertised only in languages its content exists in. Guards:
  `localized-paths.test.ts`,
  [`middleware-locale-routing.test.ts`](../../apps/web/tests/lib/middleware-locale-routing.test.ts),
  [`locale-prefix-routes.test.ts`](../../apps/web/tests/config/locale-prefix-routes.test.ts),
  [`public-funnel-english.test.tsx`](../../apps/web/tests/i18n/public-funnel-english.test.tsx),
  `tests/seo/sitemap.test.ts`. The rewrite is in
  `apps/web/src/lib/auth/middleware.ts`, which is Tier 2.
- **The funnel goes multilingual only by URL (`/<locale>/b/<slug>`), and only
  once the teacher writes her own words per language.** Never by cookie or
  header.
- **Copy goes through the catalog, and completeness is a type error.** No
  literal (`apps/web/tests/i18n-guard.test.ts`), no copy chosen by comparing
  to `"es"` or `"en"` (`tests/i18n/two-armed-copy.test.ts`,
  `tests/i18n/english-branch.test.ts`).
- **A language is added as a registry row with `launched: false`**, and is
  launched only once a native speaker has read it and its terms and privacy
  policy exist as person-made translations.
  [`launched-locales.test.ts`](../../packages/shared/src/i18n/launched-locales.test.ts)
  proves an unlaunched locale reaches no picker, redirect, hreflang set or
  sitemap.
- **A second locale of one language is a delta over its base, never a full
  catalog.** Its fallback chain is its base, then `DEFAULT_LOCALE`, never
  sideways into another language. Build the partial-catalog type first. Nothing
  enforces this yet; nothing has needed it.
- **Legal text is never in the catalog and never machine-translated.** It is
  data in `packages/shared/src/legal/`; English controls; a translation that
  falls behind the English is not shown as current
  ([`terms.test.ts`](../../packages/shared/src/legal/terms.test.ts)). Still
  owed: the controlling-language clause in each translation, which a person
  writes, and the French translations. The privacy-policy side of this is in
  [D-22](./D-22.md).

## How we got here

- **Undated, before 2026-07-15.** Web copy was inline across about 187 files in three
  styles, about half hard-coded Spanish. A shared key-based catalog with a
  compile-time completeness check replaced them, held by a ratchet against a
  baseline. The ratchet reached zero and became a ban on 2026-10-05 (#178).
- **2026-07-15.** The material generator had no subject input, so it inferred
  a language lesson for everyone, correctly for the only live teacher by
  accident. `teachers.discipline`, which named the subject, picked the tag pack
  and flagged "is it a language", became `target_language`. Two drifting lists,
  of 12 caption codes and 20 material names, became one registry generated
  from CLDR: 174 ISO 639-1 codes at the time. The product was positioned for
  language teachers only, and the music and maths packs were deleted.
- **Undated, mid-2026.** French became the third UI language; all 3,589 catalog keys
  were translated. A "System Default" preference that follows the device was
  added. French legal pages and email fell back to English.
- **2026-07-29.** The intro-video coach was found taking both its speech
  language and its feedback language from `target_language`. They now come from
  `teaching_language` and `locale`; see
  [teacher management](../features/teacher-management.md).
- **2026-08-08.** Nothing in onboarding asked for `target_language`, so new
  teachers reached the focus picker with a null and were seeded the neutral
  pack for good. Step 1 now asks, and the per-package Subject field left the
  onboarding templates step.
- **Until 2026-08-25.** The funnel was pinned to English ("students are English
  learners"), then briefly to the teacher's own `locale`. The first teacher
  falsified the second rule, and `booking_page_locale` was added.
- **Before publication (undated).** The landing eyebrow said "any language"
  while the manifest, Open Graph card and features page said "any subject". The
  copy settled on subject, and the guard was added.
- **2026-10-04.** One URL per public language, the funnel pinned with no
  switcher, one locale per language with regional deltas, and person-made legal
  translations with English controlling. The site-wide social card was one URL
  and so in one language; it became `/social-card/<locale>` the same day.
- **2026-10-05.** The `launched` field was built. The terms moved into a data
  module, and the page's rendered HTML was compared before and after for every
  language: byte-identical.

The registry now carries 184 codes (count of `code:` rows in
`languages.data.ts`, October 2026); the 174 above is the July count.
