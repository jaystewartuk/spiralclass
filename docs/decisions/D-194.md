# D-194. A visitor cannot switch a booking page's language; the teacher sets it, until she can write her own words in more than one (2026-10-04)

**Status.** Active. It restates how `/b/**` already behaves and records why,
so there is nothing to build.

**What changed.** Nothing in the code. The public booking funnel keeps
rendering in the teacher's `booking_page_locale`, through
`publicFunnelLocaleFor()`, on every surface: the pages, the client layout and
the social card. Specifically:

- `/b/**` gets no language prefix (D-193) and no `?lang=`.
- The visitor's cookie and `Accept-Language` are not read there.
- The funnel gets no language switcher.

**Why.** Half of every booking page is the teacher's own words: her headline,
her bio, her package names and descriptions, her intro video. Each is one
string in one language (`teachers.headline`, `teachers.bio`, and the package
rows). Translating the platform's half on request would give the visitor a
page in two languages: French buttons around a Spanish bio, a French checkout
for a package whose name is English. That is the split-language funnel the pin
in `locales.ts` exists to prevent. The platform must not machine-translate her
words on her behalf. They are her pitch, and a wrong translation is a promise
she did not make.

The second reason is the one the pin was built on. The social card is
rendered with no visitor present, so it can only follow a value stored on the
teacher. A per-visitor language would put a card in one language over a page
in another.

**What was considered and rejected.**

- **A prefixed URL per language (`/fr/b/ana`), each deterministic.** This
  keeps the social card deterministic and is how the funnel should go
  multilingual. But it still renders her single-language words inside a
  second language's chrome. It is the right shape once her content can exist
  in more than one language, not before.
- **Machine-translate her headline, bio and packages for the visitor.** It
  writes copy in her name that she never read. A price-bearing package
  description is the worst place for that.
- **Follow the visitor's browser, as the rest of the site does.** This is the
  rule the pin replaced, and it is what produced a funnel in two languages.
  The note above `publicFunnelLocaleFor` in `locales.ts` has the history.

**What it constrains.**

- No surface under `/b/**` reads the visitor's language. A new funnel surface
  takes its locale from `publicFunnelLocaleFor(teacher.bookingPageLocale)`
  like the three that exist.
- **What reopens this:** teacher-authored content held per language (a bio
  and headline per locale, package names per locale), written by the teacher.
  At that point the funnel goes multilingual by URL (`/<locale>/b/<slug>`),
  one deterministic language per URL. It offers only the languages she has
  written her own words in, with `hreflang` between them. Never by cookie or
  header.

**What was affected.** Nothing. The rule this restates is already enforced by
`apps/web/tests/i18n/public-funnel-english.test.tsx`.

**Trade-offs accepted, and unresolved risks.**

- **A teacher with buyers in two languages must choose one.** The first
  teacher is close to this case: she reads Spanish, and her buyers read
  English. Today the only workaround is a second account, and that is not a
  good one.
- **Nothing measures the demand.** If teachers ask for a second buyer
  language, or funnel analytics (#178 step D) show visitors leaving pages in a
  language other than their browser's, that is the evidence for building the
  per-language content model.
