# D-138. The product is renamed to `SpiralClass` and moves to `spiralclass.com` (2026-08-29)

> [!NOTE]
> **Completed 2026-08-29.** `livekit.agendaprofe.com` has been dropped from the
> box's Caddyfile and its Cloudflare DNS record deleted, so only
> `livekit.spiralclass.com` is served. ⚠️ The condition this decision set — that
> the old name go only after `LIVEKIT_URL` moved **and** a real class had
> connected on the new one — was **half met**: no class had connected (zero
> participant events in 72h). Recorded rather than glossed; the rollback path
> is in [`ORACLE_BOX_REBUILD.md`](../deployment/ORACLE_BOX_REBUILD.md) § TLS.

**Status: Active.** Supersedes the naming half of [D-133](./D-133.md) and
nothing else in it — D-133's framing half, that the product is global and the
2026-08-23 strategic reset's Spanish-market premise is withdrawn, is left
standing and is the premise this builds on. Constrains nothing new; touches
almost everything.

**What changed.** The product name is `SpiralClass` and the canonical origin is
`https://spiralclass.com`. `agendaprofe.com` is retained, not released, and
301s to the new domain. The rename ran over 934 files: brand copy, all three
i18n catalogs, the pnpm workspace names,
`APP_URL` / `BETTER_AUTH_URL`, every `*.agendaprofe.com` hostname, the docs
site, the deep-link scheme, and the logo mark.

**Why it changed.** Owner's instruction, 2026-08-29. It is a reversal by
decision rather than by evidence: D-133's stated reopen trigger — the first
paying teacher who does not read Spanish — has **not** fired, and is not
claimed to have fired. What D-133 got right is the reason this is cheap to do
today rather than expensive later, and it wrote that down itself:

> The switching cost is at its lifetime minimum today and only rises.

Every condition that made it minimal still holds on the day it was spent. Brand
equity is approximately zero at the current user base, and no published listing
has made an application id permanent. And D-133's own account of what the
name costs — _profe_ opaque to a non-Spanish speaker and pointed at a classroom
rather than an independent teacher, _agenda_ a false friend naming the
calendar, which is the least differentiated part of a product whose edge is the
AI lesson layer — is unchanged and was never disputed. D-133 deferred on the
grounds that the cost "is currently paid by nobody"; this decision accepts that
the cost was real, declines to wait for someone to pay it, and spends the
switching cost at its floor.

**What was NOT renamed, and why.** Every string that names a live external
resource or persisted client state was deliberately preserved — 329 occurrences
across 45 distinct identifiers. Renaming them buys nothing a user can see and
costs either data migration or a broken deploy:

- **Fly apps `agendaprofe` and `agendaprofe-preview`.** Fly cannot rename an
  app; a new one means new IPs, a full secrets re-push and a downtime window.
  The app name is invisible to users.
- **R2 buckets** (`agendaprofe-{,preview-,production-}{teacher-photos,teacher-videos,student-photos,class-materials,material-podcasts,recordings,chat-audio}`,
  plus `agendaprofe-tofu-state`, `-r2-state`, `-backups`, `-releases`). Bucket
  names are immutable and hold live teacher photographs, class recordings and
  the Tofu state itself.
- **GCP / Firebase project `example-project-00000`**, its
  `*.iam.gserviceaccount.com` service account and `*.firebasestorage.app` host.
  A GCP project id is immutable for the life of the project.
- **The `agendaprofe.*` client storage keys** (session token,
  cached user, device id, push token, drafts, theme, date-format preferences).
  Renaming them logs both users out and drops their preferences the first time
  a build ships.
- **The AWS IAM user `agendaprofe-ses-sender`** and its `/agendaprofe/` IAM path.
- **The Inngest app id** (`INNGEST_APP_ID`). Its own file records why: renaming
  it orphans every already-registered function until the app is manually
  re-synced on prod and preview, and that is what caused the **2026-07-07 total
  email and push outage**. A rename here is a rename plus a re-sync, in one
  change, or it is an outage.
- **The Cloudflare Worker `agendaprofe`** (`wrangler.jsonc`) serving the docs
  site, and the `agendaprofe-docs` application it deploys from. Renaming the
  Worker in config does not rename the Worker — it creates a second one and
  leaves the custom domain bound to the first.
- **The GCP service account `agendaprofe-vertex-image@`** and the Postgres
  logical replication slots `agendaprofe_region_{pub,sub}`.

Every item from the Inngest id down was caught **after** the bulk rename had
already changed it, by auditing each remaining compound token rather than
trusting the protect list. That list was built from a survey of hyphenated
tokens, so it missed everything naming a resource without a hyphen — `FLY_APP=`
among them, which is why the Fly app name survived in `-a`/`--app` form and not
in the environment-variable form three files away.

**What was affected outside code.** DNS for `spiralclass.com` was created in the
same Cloudflare account (19 records, mirroring the old zone), Fly certificates
were issued for `spiralclass.com` and `www.spiralclass.com`, and the logo mark
was redrawn — the old mark was an `A`/`P` monogram, which no longer stands for
anything. The new mark is an inward spiral on the same terracotta tile, drawn
across the same two path elements so every one of the fifteen places that
inlines it kept its structure.

**Unresolved risks.**

- **Inbound mail on the new domain does not work yet.** The three Proton
  `_domainkey` CNAMEs and the `protonmail-verification` TXT are issued per
  domain and could not be copied, so `spiralclass.com` has to be added as a
  custom domain in Proton Mail. Until that is done, **mail to
  `@spiralclass.com` does not arrive** even though its MX records already point
  at Proton. Outbound is done: `updates.spiralclass.com` was created in Resend
  (us-east-1, `send` return-path, matching the old domain) and its own DKIM key
  is published — the key copied from the old zone was wrong and was replaced.
- **`livekit.spiralclass.com` is live, and getting there corrected two beliefs
  this decision held.** Caddy on the Oracle box now serves **both** hostnames
  and holds a Let's Encrypt certificate for the new one (issued 2026-08-29,
  expires 2026-11-27); `LIVEKIT_URL` and `scripts/local/synthetic.sh` moved
  together once it existed.

  **First correction: the Tofu module was never the route.** This decision
  assumed applying `infra/oracle-runner` was how the certificate would appear.
  It is not — that module has never been applied to the live box, whose
  service configs are hand-maintained at
  `/home/ubuntu/oracle-livekit-production/`. Editing the module's
  `services/Caddyfile` changes nothing in production. The real change was a
  hand edit over Tailscale SSH plus `caddy reload`, which is graceful: the
  container never restarted and no connection was dropped. Both files now say
  so.

  **Second correction: issuance needed the record grey-clouded, and that is a
  standing renewal risk.** Caddy solved `tls-alpn-01`, which Cloudflare's proxy
  makes impossible because the edge terminates TLS — so the record had to be
  un-proxied for issuance and re-proxied after. [D-134](./D-134.md) already
  named renewal-through-the-proxy as unwatched; this is the mechanism. Caddy
  should fall back to `http-01` on renewal, which the proxy does carry, but
  **that fallback has still never actually run here** and nothing watches it.

- **The redirect broke both Stripe webhooks for 1h36m, and the redirect itself
  destroyed two pre-existing rules.** Stripe does not follow 3xx — a redirect on
  a registered endpoint is a failed delivery. Both destinations still pointed at
  `agendaprofe.com` when the redirect went in at 15:52 UTC, and neither was
  noticed until 17:28. **Nothing was lost**: Stripe reported 0 delivered and 0
  failed for the week, because there are no payers yet. The exposure was real
  and the impact was zero, which is luck rather than design.

  Three things this should have caught earlier and did not. **There are TWO
  destinations** (`/api/stripe/webhook` and `/api/stripe/billing-webhook`) with
  separate signing secrets, and the first fix excluded only one. **The redirect
  rule was written as a `PUT` to the whole entrypoint ruleset**, which silently
  deleted the zone's two pre-existing template rules ("WWW to root", "HTTP to
  HTTPS") — verified afterwards as functionally redundant, since every host
  redirects HTTP to HTTPS at its own origin, but that was checked after the fact,
  not before. And **the same class of failure had already happened once that
  afternoon** with the HetrixTools liveness monitor, which the redirect also
  broke; the lesson did not generalise from "monitors" to "anything that POSTs
  to the old domain".

  The endpoints were moved by **editing** the existing destinations, which
  preserves their ids and signing secrets, so no env var changed and no redeploy
  was needed. The redirect now permanently excludes `/api/stripe/*`.

- **Preview's `RESEND_FROM` is not symmetric with production's, and that is not
  this rename's doing.** Production moved to `soporte@updates.spiralclass.com`
  once Resend verified it. Preview stays on
  `soporte@updates.staging.agendaprofe.com` because that domain has **never**
  been a verified Resend domain — the account holds `updates.agendaprofe.com`
  and now `updates.spiralclass.com`, and nothing else. Preview's sender was
  already unverified before the rename; renaming it would only make the new
  domain look responsible for a gap that predates it. Its DNS records exist in
  the new zone, but the DKIM one there is still the copied old-domain key and
  would need replacing.
- **`docs.spiralclass.com` has no DNS record and no Access application.** The
  docs Worker's custom domain and the Cloudflare Access gate in
  `infra/cloudflare` are still bound to the old hostname; the Worker route and
  `var.domain` have to move together or the docs site is either unreachable or
  ungated. It is ungated that is the risk.
- **Decision documents D-01 to D-136 now read with the new name.** The index
  calls this log "current policy, not history", so forward-looking references
  were rewritten deliberately — but that means D-134's account of a 2026-08-2x
  incident now names `livekit.spiralclass.com`, a hostname that did not exist
  when it happened. D-133 alone is exempt and is preserved verbatim, because
  rewriting it would have made it argue for its own reversal.
- **`teachlingua.com`, D-133's costed alternative, was never evaluated against
  `spiralclass`.** The name was chosen by the owner and the domain was already
  registered on 2026-08-27, two days before this decision. No candidate search
  was re-run.
