# D-152. The teacher is told what Stripe charges her, and that we do not receive it

**Status: Active.** Copy, disclosure and guard tests. No change to how money
moves — [D-143](./D-143.md) already made that change; this is the platform
finally saying so to the person who pays.

## What changed

Under direct charges the teacher is the merchant of record. Stripe bills its
processing fee to **her own connected account**, at **her country's** rate,
and the platform never sees it. Nothing in the product said so. Six surfaces
now do, and three guard tests keep them honest.

- **`/pricing` gains a fee section** naming the two costs and who each is paid
  to: her SpiralClass plan, which is the only money we take, and Stripe's
  processing fee, which is Stripe's.
- **Settings → Billing gains the same block**, because a paying teacher lands
  there to look at a bill and every other line on that page is money that flows
  to us.
- **Settings → Payments gains it too**, rendered only where a card rail exists,
  because that is where the second bill from the second company appears.
- **The help centre says it in prose**, in `packages-and-payments`, the teacher
  FAQ and `settings-and-subscription`, in both en and es-MX. It had said nothing
  about fees at all.
- **The landing wedge note is rewritten**, and the trust pill with it.
- **The template editor stops attributing our estimate to Stripe** (below).

## Why it changed

Three separate defects, one cause: the copy was written for a platform that was
the merchant of record, and D-143 stopped being that.

- **We promised something that is not true, in five places.** "Getting paid by
  your students is always free" shipped in `/pricing`'s subtitle and meta
  description, the billing subtitles, and the billing help hint.
  It is true of SpiralClass's charge and false as a description of what it costs
  her to get paid. She now has a full Stripe dashboard in her own name and
  Stripe invoices her directly, so she **will** find the fee. Finding it after
  that sentence is the worst available order. Two of the five survived the first
  pass of this decision and were caught only by grep, which is why the rule is
  now a test rather than a habit.
- **We described a commission we no longer charge.** `cashflow.footnote` still
  told her card payments arrive lower "after Stripe's fee and the platform
  commission". One twin of that string had been corrected at D-143 and the
  other had not, so the catalog carried both the right and the wrong version of
  one sentence. `web.admin.costs.intro` and
  `web.admin.money.netProfitChartBody` had the same drift, and six
  `web.admin.money.commission*` keys were orphaned outright — nothing rendered
  them. All are fixed or removed here.
- **The one place that named a number attributed OUR number to Stripe.**
  `onboarding.templates.wiseDiscountHint` said "Stripe charges you up to
  ~5.25%". That figure is `STRIPE_WORST_CASE_RATE`, a deliberately pessimistic
  bound the platform invented to size a suggested discount — an international
  card plus the currency-conversion surcharge. Stripe UK domestic is 1.5%. It
  was wrong for nearly every teacher, and it was a price claim about a third
  party that the platform cannot stand behind.

## What the disclosure deliberately does NOT do

**It quotes no percentage on any public marketing surface.** Since D-143 the
rate is Stripe's, set per country and per payment method, and the platform has
no way to know which applies to a given teacher or a given charge. A number we
cannot stand behind, printed next to a "0% commission" claim, is exactly what
turns an honest disclosure into a misleading one. So the public copy names
**who** charges the fee, says plainly that SpiralClass receives none of it, and
links to `stripe.com/pricing`. A figure appears only in the template editor,
where one is needed to explain a suggested price, and there it is labelled as
our own high estimate and interpolated from the constant that does the
arithmetic, so the sentence cannot drift from the maths.

**The comparative claim about marketplaces is dropped.**
`web.landing.wedge.marketplace.value` carried a specific comparative price
claim about third parties with no substantiation file behind it. It now reads
"Take a cut of every class". The contrast survives; the unsupported number does
not. Restore a figure only alongside the evidence for it.

**"Teachers keep ~100%" is retired.** A tilde is not a disclosure. The pill
now claims the thing that is exactly true — `0% commission on your lessons` —
and the note beneath it carries the rest.

## The correction this decision had to make to itself

The first draft said, in six places, that **bank transfers carry no processing
fee**. That was true when the manual rail was a bank transfer the platform
brokered. It is not true now, for two reasons that landed within days of each
other:

- [D-145](./D-145.md) removed the manual `bank_account` instrument entirely.
  **Wise is the only manual rail left.**
- The same decision records that a teacher's own Stripe account began
  presenting **SPEI** and **OXXO** at checkout. A bank transfer made _inside_
  Stripe checkout is a Stripe payment and **carries Stripe's fee**.

So "bank transfer" had stopped being a synonym for "fee-free" and had become an
ambiguous term that pointed at both a fee-bearing and a fee-free rail. Every
string now names **Wise** as the rail that skips Stripe, and says that Stripe's
fee covers every method Stripe offers, cards and local options alike. The help
centre states the trap explicitly.

## Enforcement

`packages/shared/src/i18n/i18n.test.ts` gains five guards, per locale. They
guard the decision, not the wording — rephrase freely. Reverting `catalog.en.ts`
to its pre-D-152 state fails six of them, so they are not vacuous:

1. **No string charges a platform or marketplace commission.** A sentence that
   _denies_ one is fine; that is the disclosure. (This guard found
   `web.admin.costs.intro` and the net-profit chart body during development,
   neither of which the manual audit had caught.)
2. **No string hardcodes the worst-case percentage.** Where a rate appears it
   must interpolate `{rate}` from `STRIPE_WORST_CASE_RATE`.
3. **The card-fee hint must own its number** as our estimate rather than
   Stripe's rate. Scoped by key, since `{rate}` also carries an FX rate on the
   admin economics screens.
4. **The nine disclosure keys exist and are non-empty in every locale**, and
   the three bodies name Stripe.
5. **No string claims that getting paid is free.** Saying a _plan_ is free is
   fine; saying _getting paid_ is free is not, because Stripe's fee makes it
   untrue. This is the guard that would have caught the two stragglers above.

## Renames

`STRIPE_UK_*` in `packages/shared/src/pricing-fees.ts` becomes `STRIPE_*`.
The constants have not been Stripe UK's rate card since D-143 moved the charge
onto the teacher's own account, and the old name invited exactly the misreading
that shipped in the template hint. `STRIPE_PRICING_URL` and
`stripeWorstCaseRatePercent()` are added as the single source for the
disclosure's link and figure.

## Costs and open questions, stated

- **The teacher still cannot see her actual fee in SpiralClass.** The platform
  does not receive it and does not read it back from Stripe, so the honest
  answer is a pointer to her Stripe dashboard. A per-country rate table is the
  next step if this proves insufficient; `pricing-fees.ts` already flags it as
  the honest fix should the estimate ever become binding.
- **The worst-case estimate is still a single global number.** It is
  conservative in the direction that cannot cost her money, and it only sizes an
  editable suggestion, but for a UK-domestic teacher it over-estimates by more
  than 3x.
- **Nobody qualified has reviewed this copy.** [D-147](./D-147.md) already
  carries that caveat for the platform's regulatory position and it applies
  here: the "0% commission" framing plus a third-party-cost disclosure is a
  shape a solicitor should look at before any paid acquisition runs against it.
- **Nothing was measured.** There is no funnel showing the old copy cost or won
  anything, and no teacher has complained about a surprise fee. The case rests
  on the sentences being false, which is sufficient on its own.
