# D-155. A community is a place with rules, a post and a picture — not a link

**Status: Active.** Schema, generation pipeline and the Communities screen.
Builds on [D-123](./D-123.md) (social previews) and [D-125](./D-125.md)
(student acquisition); reverses neither, and reverses one thing inside D-123
that turned out to be the cause of a visible product failure.

## What changed

`/dashboard/get-students/communities` was a list of places with a tracked link
attached to each. It is now the place a teacher goes from "I want students from
this group" to "here is the post, the picture and the link, ready to paste".
Five changes, one screen.

### 1. The page answers its own question first

The list she came to read was below an add-community form, so the first
question — how many communities do I have, and what state are they in — was
answered by scrolling. The count and a promotion breakdown now sit directly
under the title, the add form folds behind a button (except when she has none,
where the form IS the empty state), and every card carries a one-line answer
for today: whether she may promote there, and if not, when she may.

### 2. Post content lives beside the link, through the entity that already exists

The screen handed her a link and stopped, which left the hardest part — what to
actually write — outside the product. Each community now has a **Post**
section: pick a kind, generate, edit, preview the finished paste (body + link +
image), copy.

**No second content pipeline.** It drives one `MarketingActivity` per
community, through the same `prepareActivity` the weekly plan runs, so a post
written here carries the same tracked link, is measured by the same results
screen, and is editable from the same place. What is new is only the entry
point: she can start from the community she is thinking about rather than from
a generated week. `getOrCreateCommunityDraft` keeps exactly one live draft per
community and retires it when she marks it done, so a post already in a feed is
never overwritten.

### 3. The image brief has three authors, and the surprised person is gone

**This is the correction.** Generated memes were, reliably, a person looking
shocked. The cause was not the model defaulting; it was us asking.
`lib/social-preview/prompt.ts` contained one hardcoded brief shared by every
teacher on the platform, and its `meme` angle read, verbatim:

> One clear human subject with an exaggerated, instantly readable facial
> expression or reaction.

D-123's reasoning for a two-field brief was sound — she picks an angle and says
what it is about, and the prompt engineering is the product's job, not hers.
What it did not anticipate is that a brief with no per-teacher input is a brief
that produces the same picture for everyone. So the brief now has three
authors, and the UI names them in those terms:

- **SpiralClass** — the rules that never change: no text in the image, a
  composition that survives a thumbnail, nothing unsafe. Not editable, but
  **explained**: a "What we'll ask for" disclosure restates the whole brief in
  her words, including those three. A teacher who cannot see why her request
  was altered concludes the tool ignored her.
- **The teacher** — `teacher_marketing_profiles.meme_brief`, written once and
  used everywhere: her niche, her humour, her audience, her red lines. Plus
  `meme_style`, a curated visual register whose default, `varied`, rotates.
- **The community** — `teacher_share_groups.meme_brief`, what lands with that
  particular audience, layered on top rather than replacing.

Three further mechanisms, all in the prompt and all tested: the forced facial
expression is **deleted**; its failure mode is now an explicit **negative**
constraint (shocked faces, stock-photo suits, thumbs-up, lightbulbs, flags as
languages), because not asking for something is not the same as forbidding it
when the model's own priors converge on it anyway; and a **variation index**,
seeded on the count of her images this month, rotates the framing and (when she
has not chosen a style) the register, so "generate another" reliably differs
rather than rolling dice that can land twice.

**Kept from D-123, deliberately:** the model still renders no text. A misspelt
Spanish meme posted by a Spanish teacher discredits the exact expertise she is
advertising, and accents are where image models fail. Her caption is drawn over
the image by Satori — free to edit, instant, and always spelled right. What
changed is that the rule is now _stated to her_ rather than merely enforced.

### 4. Images are a library she owns, not a picker repeated eight times

The same thumbnails appeared under every community with no way to remove any of
them, which read as "these belong to this community" and left every failed
generation on screen forever. There is now one **Your images** section: rename,
download, delete, and a badge saying where each one is in use. The community
panels only _choose_ among them.

Deletion resolves the `Restrict` foreign keys rather than forcing past them: a
post she already marked done **blocks** it (the picture is live in a real
feed), a draft's reference is cleared, a placement falls back to the standard
card, and the bytes go last — so a storage failure leaks an object nobody
references rather than leaving a row pointing at bytes that are gone.

### 5. "Limited" says what the limit is, and the app enforces the half it can

`promo_policy` could say `limited` and nothing more, so the teacher recorded the
coarse answer and kept the real rule in her head. Four columns now carry it,
split by what can honestly be enforced:

- **Structured, enforced deterministically.** `promo_weekdays` (empty = any
  day), `promo_every_days` (measured from the last post she marked done there),
  `promo_links_allowed` (an explicit `no` stops a tracked code being minted at
  all; an explicit `yes` still cannot open a community the policy has closed).
- **Free text, context only.** `promo_notes` rides into the generation prompt,
  fenced and labelled as quoted rules, and the UI says in as many words that we
  cannot check it for her.

The window is advisory, never a block — she read the group's rules, and a
mistyped one must not lock her out of her own community — and it is computed
against **her** timezone, because a UTC weekday tells a teacher west of
Greenwich that Monday closed on Sunday evening.

## What was affected

- **Schema.** Five nullable/defaulted columns on `teacher_share_groups`, two on
  `teacher_marketing_profiles`. One migration, no backfill: every existing row
  reads as "no rules, no brief, varied style", which is exactly today's
  behaviour.
- **`packages/shared/src/marketing/`** gains `promotion-rules.ts` (the rule
  vocabulary and the window arithmetic, pure and tested) and `meme.ts` (the
  style registry, the brief bounds, and the fixed rules in all three locales).
- **The wire contract is unchanged.** The share-groups routes keep their
  shape; the new fields are optional, and a client that omits them leaves the
  stored rules alone. `communityInputSchema` distinguishes "absent" from
  "cleared" for exactly that reason, and a hidden marker field does the same job
  for the day checkboxes, which post nothing at all when every box is cleared.
- **69 new catalog keys** in en, es-MX and fr.

## Unresolved risks

- **The rotation is a heuristic, not a guarantee.** Eight framings and five
  registers make repetition much less likely; they do not make it impossible,
  and nothing measures whether the generated images actually got better. The
  evidence for the fix is that the cause was found and removed, not that the
  output was scored.
- **Free-text rules are still unenforceable**, and a teacher may reasonably
  believe otherwise however plainly the help text says so. The structured rules
  exist to bound how much can go wrong on that assumption.
- **One draft per community** is a product opinion. A teacher who wants two
  posts prepared for one group has to finish one first, and switching the kind
  rewrites the row rather than branching.
- **The frequency rule counts only posts she marks done.** A teacher who posts
  without marking it will be told she may promote when her group's rules say
  otherwise — the app cannot see a Facebook feed, and guessing would be worse.
