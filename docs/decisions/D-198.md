# D-198. A caption shows the words as they are spoken, and the translation replaces them when the phrase ends (2026-10-12)

**Status.** Active, built 2026-10-12. Narrowed by [D-206](./D-206.md)
(2026-10-27): a reader of a translation gets a wordless partial, never the
speaker's untranslated words.

**What changed.** Every live-caption recogniser now reports the utterance it
is still hearing, not only finished ones: the browser recogniser runs with
`interimResults = true`, and the Deepgram fallback opens with
`interim_results=true`. Those words go to the listener at once, untranslated,
as a `CaptionLine` with `partial: true` under one id that grows as the
recogniser hears more. When the utterance finishes it is translated exactly as
before, and the finished line takes the partial's id, so it replaces the words
in place.

Three things do not change:

- **Only finished utterances are translated.** A partial is never sent to the
  translator, on the device or the server, so the translation bill and the
  route's rate limits are what they were.
- **Only finished utterances are kept** for the lesson transcript (D-189).
- **A failed translation is still dropped, never shown as one.** The listener
  has already seen its words as they were said.

A translation now also gives up after `TRANSLATE_TIMEOUT_MS` (6 s), because
one speaker's lines are shown in the order spoken and a request left to hang
held every later line of theirs behind it.

**Why.** A teacher reported, on 2026-10-12, that her new student "heard me and
did not see the subtitles, and then they appeared later", and found it
confusing. Measured from production logs for that class: the teacher was on an
iPhone, so the student's Mac recognised the teacher's speech from the call
audio and translated it Spanish → English through the server. 95 of the
teacher's lines were translated. The median was 14 characters, the 90th
percentile 111 and the longest 499, which is about half a minute of speech.
Every one of those characters was on nobody's screen until the phrase ended.
The translation itself was not the cause: the translate route answered in
0.1–0.3 s for nearly every line, with two outliers of 1.9 s and 2.7 s.

The day before, the same teacher's class with another student had no
complaint. There, only the student's speech was translated (206 lines). The
teacher's speech reached that student untranslated. The delay is the
finished-utterance rule, and it is felt most where a listener reads a
translation of someone who talks in long runs. A teacher explaining
something is exactly that case.

D-27 decided "only finalized utterances are translated — interim partials are
not — to keep the on-screen text clean English rather than half-translated
Spanish". That still holds: nothing half-translated is shown. What changed
is the premise that a clean line late is better than the speaker's own words
now. In a language lesson the words the speaker is saying are the lesson, and
the band already shows the original above the translation (CaptionLine.src)
for that reason.

**What was considered and rejected.**

- **Translating partials as well.** Each word would trigger a translation of
  the whole phrase so far. That multiplies the server translation bill and the
  route's per-caller budget by the number of revisions, and the result is the
  "half-translated" text D-27 rejected, rewritten under the reader's eye.
- **Forcing shorter finals** by stopping and restarting recognition every few
  seconds. Chrome does not offer a "finish now". A restart leaves a gap of at
  least 250 ms in which speech is lost (recognition-policy.ts), and the cut
  falls mid-sentence, so the translation gets worse.
- **Showing partials only on the device that recognises them.** The other
  participant is the reader, so that would have fixed nothing for her.

**What it constrains.**

- A partial must stay untranslated and must stay out of the transcript. Both
  are asserted in `tests/captions/browser-captions.test.ts`.
- The feed must never let a partial overwrite the finished line with the same
  id (`reduceCaptionFeed`). A late partial would put untranslated words back
  over a translation.
- A partial line is `aria-hidden`, and the finished line mounts as a new
  element, so the band's `role="log"` announces the finished line with its
  translation. It does not announce a word-by-word stream.
- A partial carries only the newest `PARTIAL_TAIL_CHARS` (140) of the
  utterance. The reader needs the words arriving now, and the packet stays
  small however long the speaker goes.

**What was affected.** `packages/shared/src/captions.ts` (`partial` on the
wire, decoded only when exactly `true`), `packages/shared/src/caption-recognition.ts`
(`buildPartialCaptionLine`, `partialTail`, an `id` for `buildCaptionLine`),
`apps/web/src/lib/captions/` (`recognizer.ts`, `cloud-recognizer.ts`,
`deepgram-live.ts`, `browser-captions.ts`, `caption-translator.ts`,
`caption-feed.ts`), `apps/web/src/components/video/caption-band.tsx`, and
[live-calls-video.md](../features/live-calls-video.md).

**Trade-offs accepted, and unresolved risks.**

- **A reader of the translation still waits for the end of a phrase for the
  translation itself.** What she gets now is the speaker's words in the
  meantime. A long run of speech is still translated as one line when it
  ends.
- **The other participant's browser now receives several packets a second
  while someone speaks**, over LiveKit's reliable data channel. Each packet is
  at most ~140 characters of text. This has not been measured on a weak phone
  connection.
- **A partial whose utterance never finishes** (the recogniser restarts
  mid-phrase, or its translation fails) stays as untranslated text until its
  lifetime ends. It also stays in the in-call transcript panel as it was last
  heard.
- **An older client** renders a partial as an ordinary line, and the finished
  line replaces it, the way a redelivery is replaced. Nothing breaks, but the
  partial is not marked as provisional on that client.
- **Nothing measures caption latency.** The numbers above came from server
  logs of translate calls, and a class that needs no translation leaves no log
  at all. Instrumenting speech-end-to-screen is not done here.
