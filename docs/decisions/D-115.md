# D-115. Every cron fires on minute `:00`, and a job that needs sub-hourly precision arms a delayed event instead

Every cron in the fleet fires on minute `:00` and no other minute, and none
fires more than once an hour. The class reminders still land at the exact
moment. Each `reminder-scan` run arms one delayed `reminder.due` event for the
moment the next reminder comes due, and that event re-runs the same scan
(`scanAndScheduleNextWake` in
[`reminder-scan.ts`](../../apps/web/src/lib/notifications/reminder-scan.ts)).
A new booking re-runs the scan too
([`on-booking-created.ts`](../../apps/web/src/lib/inngest/functions/on-booking-created.ts)).

## Why

Neon's Free plan suspends compute only after five idle minutes, and that timer
cannot be changed. Every touch of the database therefore buys at least five
minutes of billed compute. Ticks that share a minute share one wake window. So
the monthly spend is set by how many distinct minutes the fleet uses, not by
how much work the jobs do. On the old `*/15` grid this came to about 89 of the
100 free CU-hours a month, with essentially no users. About 72% of that was
the idle timer.

## Options considered

- **Lower `max_cu`.** This saves nothing, because the compute measured at the
  0.25 floor and never scaled up.
- **Merge the seven `*/15` jobs into one function.** This is worth about 1.1 h
  a day at most, because cost follows tick count, not function count.
- **Drop the 15-minute reminder.** This is cheap, but students lose the "get
  to your desk" nudge. It was the only reason for a sub-hourly cron, and on its
  own it cost about 45 CU-hours a month.
- **An hourly grid plus a delayed wake at the exact due moment.** This was
  chosen. It keeps the reminder and pays for one window an hour.

## What it cost

- **`auto-complete-sweep` lag widened from about 15 to about 60 minutes.** It
  moves no money. It flips a booking to `completed`, so the lag lands on the
  earned-versus-held split of the cash-flow dashboard. A teacher's own Wise
  auto-confirmation, Google Calendar sync and the other backstops also run
  hourly now.
- **The chain uses durable timers.** A wake is at most about 65 minutes out,
  carries **no booking identity**, and is re-derived by the hourly cron. If
  every in-flight wake were lost, the cost would be at most an hour of
  precision, and it would fix itself on the next tick. A cancelled or moved
  class is simply not due when its wake lands.
- **The `enqueue()` seam now honours `startAfter` and `singletonKey` on
  Inngest too,** as the event's `ts` and its `id`. Inngest's `id` deduplicates
  for 24 hours. A duplicate that slips through costs one idempotent scan.

## What it constrains

- **A cron may fire only on minute `:00`.** A daily `30 16 * * *` silently opens
  a second window. `WAKE_GRID = [0]` in
  [`crons.test.ts`](../../apps/web/tests/jobs/crons.test.ts) fails on any other
  minute. A job that needs sub-hourly precision should arm a delayed event, not
  tighten the grid for every job.
- **The tightest reminder is 15 minutes before class.** A leg closer than
  about five minutes cannot be made affordable, because its fire moments would
  sit inside the five-minute suspend timer.
- **The wake carries no booking identity.** It only says "re-run the scan at
  time T", which is why a stale wake cannot fire a wrong reminder.
- **Preview registers no crons and does not run `on-booking-created`**
  (`productionOnlyEventFunctions` in
  [`index.ts`](../../apps/web/src/lib/inngest/functions/index.ts)). This keeps
  any reminder from firing there.
  [`preview-cron-gate.test.ts`](../../apps/web/tests/inngest/preview-cron-gate.test.ts)
  enforces it.
- **`JOBS_BACKEND=pgboss` would spend the whole allowance on its own.**
  `lib/jobs/boss.ts` sets `useListenNotify: true`, so a LISTEN connection
  never lets compute suspend. That is about 180 CU-hours a month, and the free
  plan suspends compute outright around day 17. Before that switch, choose
  either a non-Postgres queue or a paid plan. Nothing guards the flag.

## How we got here

- **2026-08-08.** The tightest reminder moved from 5 to 15 minutes before
  class, because consecutive 5-minute fire moments sat inside the suspend
  timer.
- **August 2026, measured.** Neon production was measured over 23 hours:
  - 87 wake windows, 81 of them exactly on `:00/:15/:30/:45`
  - 10.55 active hours of 22.75 wall-clock hours (46%)
  - average 0.2575 CU, which is the 0.25 floor
  - a median window of 5.7 minutes, so about 0.7 minutes of real work per tick

  That projects to about 11.1 active hours a day, or 89 CU-hours a month. Of
  that, 96 ticks × 5 minutes = 8.0 h a day was idle timer.

- **August 2026, the change.** Seven `*/15` jobs moved to `0 * * * *`:
  `reminder-scan`, `auto-complete-sweep`, `poll-push-receipts`,
  `poll-wise-statements`, `reconcile-paid-payments`, `redispatch-queued` and
  `sync-google-calendars`. Two daily `:30` nudges moved to 17:00 and 18:00 UTC.
  The projection fell to about 2.8 active hours a day, or about 22 CU-hours a
  month. The `auto-complete-sweep` cadence had been tightened to `*/15`
  earlier for the dashboard figure. That tightening cost a quarter of the
  allowance, so it was reversed.
- **2026-08-16, the gap.** A wake was only ever armed by a scan, and nothing
  scanned when a booking was created. A class booked at 10:10 for 11:30 got
  its 1-hour reminder at 11:00, 30 minutes late. A teacher's self-serve booking
  at 10:10 for 10:50 never got its 15-minute reminder at all. Under `*/15` the
  worst case had been 15 minutes late. Since then `booking.created` re-runs
  the scan. This costs no extra wake window, because the booking has just
  written to the database. The precision claim above holds only with this in
  place.
