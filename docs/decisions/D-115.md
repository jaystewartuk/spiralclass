# D-115. Collapse the cron wake grid to hourly; serve the 15m reminder leg with a delayed-event wake chain instead of a faster poll

**What changed.** Every cron in the fleet now fires on minute `:00` and nothing
else. The seven `*/15` jobs (`reminder-scan`, `auto-complete-sweep`,
`poll-push-receipts`, `poll-wise-statements`, `reconcile-paid-payments`,
`redispatch-queued`, `sync-google-calendars`) moved to `0 * * * *`, and the two
daily `:30` nudges moved to their own whole hour (`package-consumed-nudge`
15:30→17:00 UTC, `homework-overdue-nudge` 16:30→18:00 UTC). The precision the
`*/15` grid existed to buy is preserved for the one job that needed it: each
`reminder-scan` run now computes the exact moment the next reminder leg comes
due and arms a single delayed `reminder.due` event at that instant, which
re-enters the same scan (`scanAndScheduleNextWake` in
`apps/web/src/lib/notifications/reminder-scan.ts`, handled by
`on-reminder-due.ts` on Inngest and the `reminder.due` queue on pg-boss). The
`enqueue()` seam was extended so its existing `startAfter`/`singletonKey`
options are honoured on the Inngest path too, mapped onto the event payload's
`ts` (future-dated events are held until then) and `id` (24h idempotency key) —
previously the seam documented these as pg-boss-only, which was wrong.

**Why it likely changed.** Measured against the live Neon production project
(the production project, branch `production`) over 23 hours: 87 wake windows,
81 of them exactly on the `:00/:15/:30/:45` grid; 10.55 active hours of 22.75
wall (46% duty); average 0.2575 CU, i.e. pinned at the 0.25 floor and never
autoscaling. That projects to ~11.1 active h/day ≈ **89 of the 100 free
CU-hours/month**, with essentially no real users on the system.

The decomposition is what forced the design. Neon's Free-plan scale-to-zero
timer is a fixed, unconfigurable 5 minutes, so `96 ticks/day × 5 min = 8.0
h/day` — 72% of the entire spend — is idle timer, not work. The measured median
window was 5.7 min, so all seven crons' combined real work was ~0.7 min per
tick. Cost is therefore set by **tick count alone**: not query cost, not
`max_cu` (lowering it saves nothing when the compute never scales up), and not
consolidating the seven functions into one (worth ~1.1 h/day at most).

Six of the seven `*/15` jobs are latency-insensitive backstops whose own source
comments already said so. Only `reminder-scan` needed a sub-hourly cadence, and
only for the 15-minute "get to your desk" student nudge — meaning that single
nudge was costing ~45 CU-hours/month, roughly half the free allowance. Dropping
the leg was the cheap fix; arming a delayed event at the exact due moment keeps
the nudge and still gets the hourly grid, so the product gave up nothing.

**What was affected.**

- **Compute:** 96 ticks/day → 24, projecting ~2.8 active h/day ≈ **~22
  CU-hours/month** (from ~89), plus a handful of extra wakes on days that
  actually have classes. Preview is unaffected — it registers no crons (see
  `inngest/functions/index.ts`) and was already idle.
- **Reminder precision is unchanged**, and is now exact rather than
  grid-rounded: a class at an arbitrary minute gets its 15m leg at
  `start − 15m`, which no `*/15` tick ever hit either.
- **`auto-complete-sweep` lag widens from ~15 min to ~60 min.** This is the one
  real product regression. It flips a booking's status and reclassifies revenue
  already collected — it moves no money — so the lag lands on the earned-vs-held
  split of the cash-flow dashboard and nowhere else. Phase 2b-ii had tightened
  it to `*/15` for exactly that number; that tightening cost a quarter of the
  free allowance and is reversed here.
- **Wise auto-confirmation** (for teachers who connected their own Wise API
  credentials) now lands within the hour rather than the quarter-hour.
- **The wake chain introduces durable timers, which Phase 2b-ii deliberately
  deleted** — but not the kind it deleted. That was a multi-day `sleepUntil`
  fan-out armed at `booking.created`, orphanable at the Inngest→pg-boss cutover.
  A wake is at most ~65 minutes out, carries **no booking identity**, and is
  re-derived by the hourly cron regardless. Losing every in-flight wake costs at
  most one hour of precision and self-heals on the next tick.
- **Stale wakes cannot mis-fire.** Because the event says only "re-run the scan
  at time T", a class cancelled or rescheduled after its wake was armed is
  simply not due when the wake lands, and the re-scan arms the next moment off
  the live rows. This is the property that made the due-scan design worth
  keeping, and it is preserved intact.
- `tests/jobs/crons.test.ts`'s `WAKE_GRID` guard tightened from
  `[0, 15, 30, 45]` to `[0]`. Its failure message now points at the delayed-event
  escape hatch, so the next job needing sub-hourly precision arms a wake rather
  than charging every other job for one job's cadence.

**Unresolved risks.**

- **`JOBS_BACKEND=pgboss` would blow the entire allowance on its own, and this
  change does not address that.** `lib/jobs/boss.ts` sets
  `useListenNotify: true`, so the worker holds a permanent LISTEN connection and
  the endpoint never suspends: ~100% duty ≈ 180 CU-hours/month, exhausting the
  quota around day 17 of a billing period — and on the Free plan that is a hard
  compute suspend, not an overage bill. The audit's Phase 3 cutover needs either
  a non-Postgres queue or a paid Neon plan decided first. Nothing in the repo
  currently guards the flag against this.
- Wake dedup leans on Inngest's `id` idempotency having a 24h window. Wakes are
  always ≤65 min out so the margin is large, but a duplicate that slipped
  through would only cost one extra scan (idempotent) plus one extra 5-minute
  wake.
- The 15m leg remains the floor of what this design can serve cheaply. A leg
  tighter than ~5 minutes cannot be made affordable by any scheduling trick,
  because consecutive fire moments would sit closer together than the 5-minute
  suspend timer — the reason the leg moved 5m→15m on 2026-08-08 still stands.
- Off-grid wakes from real traffic (~6/day measured) are untouched and will grow
  with real usage. That is the intended shape: users waking the database is what
  the budget is for; crons waking it for nothing is what this removes.

**Addendum (2026-08-16).** A between-ticks gap was found in the wake chain as
shipped: a wake was only ever armed _by_ a scan, and scans ran only on the
hourly tick or on a wake — nothing ran one when a booking was created. So a
booking created between ticks whose next leg fell before the next tick had no
wake armed for it. Concretely, a class booked at 10:10 for 11:30 has its 1h leg
due at 10:30, but nothing fired until the 11:00 tick — the "one hour before"
reminder landed 30 minutes before class (up to 60 min late in general); and a
class booked at 10:10 for 10:50 (a teacher's self-serve booking bypasses the
min-advance window) has its 15m leg due at 10:35, which the 11:00 tick excludes
outright (`scheduledStart <= now`) — that reminder never fired. Under the old
`*/15` grid the worst case had been 15 min late. Fixed by
`apps/web/src/lib/inngest/functions/on-booking-created.ts`: an event function on
`booking.created` (which every booking-creation path already emits) that
re-runs the same `scanAndScheduleNextWake` — idempotent, no extra Neon wake
window since the DB was just written to, still carrying no booking identity —
so the leg's wake is armed at creation and the hourly cron is the backstop. It
is registered on production only (`productionOnlyEventFunctions` in
`inngest/functions/index.ts`), because preview keeps its crons off precisely so
no reminder ever fires there and this function starts the same chain. The
"Reminder precision is unchanged" claim in the body above holds only with this
addendum in place.
