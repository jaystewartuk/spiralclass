# D-140. Legibility is the governing constraint: a dyslexia-first design system (2026-08-29)

**Status: Active.** Extends the existing brand-token layer —
`packages/shared/src/tokens.ts`, the one canonical source both clients read —
by replacing every value in it and adding the roles it lacked. That
architecture is unchanged; this is a values-and-rules change, not a rewrite.

## The decision

**Reading comfort outranks visual identity.** Every typographic and colour rule
below is set by what helps a dyslexic reader, and the brand is expressed in what
is left over — which turns out to be plenty. The single most important mechanism
is not a font: it is that **the reader sets text size, letter and word spacing,
and background tint, and the choice persists.**

## Why reader control is the mechanism

Dyslexia does not present one way. Preferred spacing, size and ground vary
enough between people that any fixed choice is a compromise for most of them. So
the system stops guessing: it ships defaults set to the evidence-led baseline,
and exposes three controls that actually change the page. Everything else here
is about making the untouched default good enough that most people never open
them.

This is a new persisted user preference and a new settings surface. It is the
most expensive item in this decision and the one with the clearest justification.

## What the evidence supports, and what it does not

**Rejected: a dyslexia-branded typeface.** OpenDyslexic and Dyslexie are the
recognisable answer. Controlled studies have repeatedly failed to show a reading
speed or accuracy advantage for either over an ordinary well-drawn sans-serif;
some readers report preferring them, which is worth acknowledging but is not the
same as performance. Shipping one would be doing the legible-looking thing rather
than the legible one.

**Chosen: Atkinson Hyperlegible**, drawn by the Braille Institute for low
vision. Its mechanism is the one that transfers: characters that are commonly
confused are given genuinely different shapes rather than mirrored or repeated
ones — `b d p q` are not rotations of a single form, and `I l 1` are three
distinct strokes rather than the same vertical. It is also not a face anyone
would reach for by default, which incidentally solves the problem
[the previous direction](#what-this-reverses) had.

**Structural rules, which carry more weight than the typeface choice:**

- Letter spacing and word spacing opened by default. Increased spacing has
  better support in the literature than any font substitution.
- Line height 1.7; measure capped near 62 characters.
- Left aligned, never justified — justification creates uneven word gaps and
  vertical rivers.
- **No italics.** Slanted text measurably slows dyslexic readers; emphasis is
  weight.
- **No all-caps labels.** Capitals remove the word-shape cue, and the previous
  direction used uppercase micro-labels throughout.
- Body text no smaller than 17px, supporting text no smaller than 15px. The
  current app has 48 arbitrary sizes below `text-xs`.
- Status is never carried by hue alone — every state has a word in it.
- Targets at least 44px, borders 2px, focus rings 3px.

**Grounds are never pure white and text is never pure black.** Body text is
solved to **12.8:1** — comfortably past the AAA threshold of 7:1, and
deliberately short of black-on-white's 21:1, the pairing most often reported as
causing glare and apparent letter movement. A warm tint is available as a reader
control rather than imposed.

## What this reverses

Two choices from the previous direction in this same document's history were
actively harmful and are withdrawn:

- **The width axis.** Archivo was chosen partly because its condensed axis
  solved French text expansion in fixed-width navigation. Condensing tightens
  exactly the letter spacing that helps a struggling reader. French is now
  handled by wrapping and shorter labels, not by narrowing the face.
- **Uppercase tracked micro-labels**, used for every label, table header and
  eyebrow.

Also withdrawn: negative letter-spacing on headings, and italics as emphasis.

## What survives every revision

The near-monochrome ground with a single reserved hue, and the method
underneath. Visual noise costs a struggling reader more than anyone else, so
roughly ninety percent of the interface stays neutral and one saturated teal is
reserved for the primary action and for presence — a class happening now.

Every colour is derived by binary search over OKLCH lightness for the smallest
step clearing its required ratio against the hardest surface in its own theme,
where "hardest" is computed by luminance rather than assumed. **62 pairings
verify across both themes with zero failures.**

The original diagnosis is unchanged across all three directions: the old palette
was authored as twenty-nine appearances and then assigned roles, with contrast
treated as something to check afterwards, which nobody did. Measuring it found
four roles it claimed and could not perform — gold at 2.16:1 that could not
carry text, dark-mode error text at 4.19:1, control borders at 1.39:1 in a
product of roughly ninety forms, and `primary` at 3.48:1 on any raised card.

## Cost accepted

- **Less fits on a screen.** A 17px floor, 1.7 line height and opened spacing
  make every dense surface longer — the calendar, the admin tables, the student
  detail page. That is the trade, taken deliberately.
- **Atkinson Hyperlegible ships two weights.** Hierarchy comes from size,
  colour and space rather than from a weight ramp. Workable, and a real
  constraint on how much nuance the type system can carry.
- **A new persisted preference and settings surface**, with the storage,
  server-side rendering and cross-device questions that implies.
- **Any client pinned to an older build** consumes the same tokens and
  changes without being rebuilt or tested. Accepted: forking the token source
  would defeat what a single token layer
  exists to prevent.
- **The warmth of the original identity is gone**, and a cool off-white ground
  is a quieter first impression on the marketing page.

## What this does not decide

- **The D-122 breakpoint scale.** `sm` and `md` are both 1281px, so every width
  from 640 to 1280 gets the phone layout, and the baseline shows the dashboard
  using under half the width at 1920.
- **The wordmark case** — `spiralclass` in the header, `SpiralClass` everywhere
  else.
- **Whether the reader controls belong in settings, in a persistent toolbar, or
  both.** Only that they exist and persist.

Evidence: `docs/design/` — 1,164 captures taken 2026-08-29 before any token
moved, and `pnpm design:drift`'s baseline of 441 bypasses across 102 files.

## Correction, 2026-08-29 — the floor was stated but never held

This decision's own legibility rule was violated by the system built to
implement it, and the measurement said the opposite. Recorded here rather than
quietly fixed, because the shape of the mistake is more useful than the fix.

**What the rule says.** Body text no smaller than 17px, supporting text no
smaller than 15px.

**What shipped.** A seven-step scale whose `body` was 16px and whose smallest
step, `label`, was 11px. The migration that "deleted the shadow scale under the
type scale" moved 47 arbitrary sizes — 10px and 9px among them — onto that 11px
step. The drift count for arbitrary font sizes went from 47 to 0 and was
reported as a win. **Not one of those 47 pieces of text got larger.** An
unnamed violation had been converted into a named one, which is worse: the name
made it look decided.

**And wiring the scale into Tailwind did not replace Tailwind's own scale, it
sat beside it.** The app went on using the stock one — `text-sm` in 802 places
and `text-xs` in 499 — which render 14px and 12px. So the floor was true only
of the steps almost nothing used, across roughly 1,300 call sites.

**Three guards were watching and none of them fired:**

- The type-scale test asserted every step was at least as large as
  `typeScale.label.fontSize` — a tautology, since nothing in a scale can be
  smaller than its own smallest step. It certified the exact violation it
  existed to catch.
- The drift scanner counts _arbitrary_ values. A named token at the wrong size
  is invisible to it by construction, so the migration looked like progress on
  the only axis being measured.
- The theme-parity test compares the palette against the CSS mirror. Both
  encoded an inverted dark surface ladder, and agreeing with each other was all
  it checked. (Found the same day, same shape.)

**The fix.** `label` is deleted rather than raised — its 48 call sites take
`small`, and the scale now has no step beneath the floor it claims. `body`
moves to 17 and `h3` to 19 above it. Tailwind's stock keys are _redefined_ onto
the same floors (`xs` and `sm` both 15, `base` 17, `lg` 19) rather than
removed, because removing them would make 1,300 class names silently emit
nothing — Tailwind does not error on a class it cannot resolve. Nothing
shrinks; every value is the same or larger.

**The cost is the one this decision already accepted:** less fits on a screen.
That cost is now actually being paid, which it was not before.

**The general lesson, since it recurred three times in one day:** a guard that
derives its bound from the thing it is guarding proves only that the thing
equals itself. Each of the three had to be re-pointed at an external
constant — the literal 17 and 15 here, the WCAG ratio in the contrast test, the
luminance ordering in the surface test.
