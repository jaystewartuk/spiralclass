# D-185. Captions are recognised in the participants' browsers, and the lesson transcript is that caption text

Live captions run in the browsers, not on a server. Each person's speech is
recognised by their own browser when it can recognise during a call (today,
desktop Chromium with the on-device speech API), otherwise by the other
person's browser from the call audio, and only when neither can — two phones —
by the speaker's browser streaming its call microphone track to Deepgram.
Finished utterances are translated on the device where a translator exists,
otherwise by `POST /api/captions/translate` (Google Cloud Translation). With
the transcription flag on and the pairing's recorded insights consent, those
finished lines are kept, text only, as the class's `LessonTranscript`, which
the post-class insights pipeline reads. No audio is captured for it.

## Why

There is no machine for a captions server. The free `e2-micro` production
video runs on carries the call and nothing else ([D-16](./D-16.md)); an agent
on LiveKit Cloud is a third participant billed per minute — at the operator's
target of 32 fifty-minute 1:1 classes a month (3,200 participant-minutes),
about 1,600 more, roughly 4,800 of the free 5,000. Browsers now ship what such
a server provided: recognition that accepts a `MediaStreamTrack` and, on
desktop Chrome, on-device translation. The operator asked for the best free
option.

Insights had begun with a LiveKit egress per speaker, which that box cannot
carry, and every way back to egress costs money. Captions already produce a
speaker-labelled, time-ordered stream of finished lines — everything the
analysis reads (`speaker`, `text`, `startMs`).

## Options considered

- **A server-side agent** elsewhere (another VM, LiveKit Cloud's agents) —
  money, capacity the free tiers lack, or a rewrite, plus the third
  participant's minutes.
- **Only the speaker's own browser** — Android speakers would never be
  captioned, and nobody knows in advance who joins from a phone.
- **Pausing the call's microphone on Android** — measured: nothing let Android
  recognise while the call holds the mic.
- **Paid streaming speech-to-text for everyone** — a per-minute cost on every
  class; kept for two phones only.
- **Recognition in WebAssembly** (Whisper, Vosk) — a model per language, heavy
  on phones, untested.
- **Translating partial results** — multiplies the translation bill and
  per-caller budget by the number of revisions, and shows half-translated
  text rewriting itself.
- **Browser audio to R2, then Deepgram batch, for the transcript** — about
  $0.43 per 50-minute class at $0.0043 a minute, forever. Still the path if
  pronunciation scoring is wanted, since that needs audio.
- **A second recogniser for insights, independent of the captions toggle** —
  silent recognition with no indicator participants understand.

## What it cost

- **Insights only run for a captioned class.**
- **Accuracy is the recogniser's, unmeasured on learner speech**; both
  recognisers tend to tidy grammar. The teacher-confirm loop bounds it.
- **Pronunciation scoring stays dark** (it needs audio and Azure).
- **Speech goes to the browser vendor** (Google, for Chrome) unless the
  on-device model is installed, which happens only on the teacher's computer;
  a student's speech may be recognised by the teacher's browser. The privacy
  notice says both.
- **Attribution and timings are the browser's claim.** A participant could
  label lines as the other speaker; durations are the gap since that
  speaker's last line, capped at a minute; a lost POST is a lost line; a break
  long enough to close the room finalises the class.
- **Paid paths.** Google Translation bills past 500,000 characters a month
  ($20 per million, checked 2026-09-23). Deepgram Nova-3 streaming was $0.0048
  a minute on promotion, $0.0077 regular (operator, 2026-09-23): a two-phone
  50-minute class with consent is about 100 minutes, $0.48–0.77. Vendor
  usage alerts are the authoritative measure; the routes cannot see socket
  length.
- **iOS, Safari and Edge were not measured**, and "has the on-device API"
  stands in for "honours `start(track)`", which the spec cannot detect.

## What it constrains

- **API presence is not the capability.** `canRecognizeDuringCall`
  ([`caption-recognition.ts`](../../packages/shared/src/caption-recognition.ts))
  claims only desktop Chromium with the on-device API; widen it only after
  measuring a browser in a live call.
- **Never two paths for one speaker, and no server path beside the browsers.**
  `/api/captions/stt-token` re-reads the room from LiveKit and refuses when a
  participant can recognise, the teacher's `captionsOn` is not `"true"`, or the
  caller is alone (`apps/web/tests/captions/stt-token-route.test.ts`).
- **The client never names a language or model**; the routes resolve both from
  the booking, or the platform becomes a general translation service.
- **Long-lived keys never reach a browser**: Deepgram gets a 30-second grant
  token, rate-limited and logged without audio or token. Stay opted out of
  Deepgram's model training (`mip_opt_out=true`,
  [`deepgram-live.ts`](../../apps/web/src/lib/captions/deepgram-live.ts)).
- **The teacher's toggle is the room's one switch**, covering both people.
  The student is captioned only with her recorded captions consent (her
  guardian's for a minor, [D-22](./D-22.md)), enforced in `assignRecognizers`
  and again by every route. Browsers re-read consent, languages and plan each
  minute (`POST /api/captions/config`). The token grant keeps
  `canUpdateOwnMetadata`, which the participant attributes need.
- **On-device downloads start inside the toggle click, translator first**
  (`installOnDeviceModels`, `apps/web/tests/captions/recognizer.test.ts`).
- **Deepgram's streaming host stays in CSP `connect-src`**
  (`apps/web/tests/lib/csp.test.ts`).
- **A partial stays untranslated, out of the transcript, and never overwrites
  its finished line**; it carries the newest 140 characters
  (`PARTIAL_TAIL_CHARS`) and is `aria-hidden`. A translation gives up after
  6 s and a failed one is dropped, never shown as one
  (`browser-captions.test.ts`, `caption-feed.test.ts` under
  `apps/web/tests/captions/`).
- **Keeping a line is decided on the server, per class, for both speakers**:
  `lessonTranscriptCaptureOk`
  ([`browser-transcript.ts`](../../apps/web/src/lib/transcription/browser-transcript.ts));
  `browser-live` is a lifecycle state, and any second completion path goes
  through `finalizeBrowserTranscript`.
- **Text only, never audio, for insights.** Capturing audio reopens the
  privacy policy, the supplier register and this record. The egress capture
  requires both the transcription flag and recording; do not loosen it
  (`apps/web/tests/video/call-recording.test.ts`).
- **The indicator stays** ([`learning-notes-indicator.tsx`](../../apps/web/src/components/video/learning-notes-indicator.tsx)):
  quieter is fine, invisible is not.
- **Insights are analysed after class, and only teacher-confirmed findings
  count** (`apps/web/tests/lesson-insights/phase-e.integration.test.ts`). No
  real-time AI analysis besides captions. Speech recognition and pronunciation
  scoring are bought behind swappable seams, not built.
- **Every AI capability needs an explicit flag AND its vendor key**, so a
  credential appearing in the secrets manager switches nothing on. Preview's
  flags follow production's. A flag turns a capability on; consent decides
  whose speech is kept.
  [`ai-marketing-claims.test.ts`](../../apps/web/tests/config/ai-marketing-claims.test.ts)
  pins the flags against the privacy policy and the marketing claims.

## How we got here

- **Before July 2026 (undated) — from the teacher's browser.** The app ran on
  a serverless host that could not hold a worker, so the teacher's browser
  streamed her microphone to Deepgram and finished lines were translated by
  Anthropic's fast model, Spanish to English, one way.
- **2026-07-25 — a server-side agent** on the Oracle box, one Deepgram stream
  per participant. Live testing on 2026-07-26 found the token lacked
  `canUpdateOwnMetadata`; timing logs showed translation was nearly all the
  latency (about 890 ms average, up to 1.7 s).
- **2026-07-28 — a two-day silent outage**: the teacher saw no captions while
  the student did, because the agent image predated the teacher-only toggle
  and nothing reported what was deployed. From 2026-07-29 it shipped from a
  git checkout on the box with SHA-pinned images (a nine-minute build on both
  OCPUs).
- **2026-09-19 — the box was lost** ([D-16](./D-16.md)); the toggle stayed
  visible while the agent ran nowhere.
- **2026-09-23 — captions moved into the browsers.** A harness mirroring the
  call's capture, Chrome 153 on a MacBook Air and a Galaxy S25 Ultra: desktop
  Chrome recognised during a call, ran two recognisers in one tab and kept
  working hidden; Android Chrome fired `audiostart` but never `speechstart` in
  45 seconds of speech while the call held the mic. One click installed both
  on-device models, translator first (130 ms and 6.5 s). The two-phone
  Deepgram fallback came the same day, and every device combination then
  captioned on production (issue #115). Still unverified: whether
  `mip_opt_out` changes Deepgram's price.
- **Transcription flag**: on 2026-07-21; off 2026-08-08 before the first
  promotion, when audio was found reaching R2 before the transcription gate
  and podcasts got a flag (they had been gated only by a key); on 2026-08-26;
  off 2026-09-24 with egress gone ([D-16](./D-16.md)).
- **2026-09-28 — the transcript became the caption text** and the flag came
  back on. PostHog `call_connected`, 30 days to 2026-09-28: about 19 of 28
  bookings had the student on Mac or Windows Chrome (free); about 7 had a
  teacher on tablet or iPhone and a student on a phone (Deepgram, already
  paid for captions, with about $192 of free credit then).
- **2026-10-12 — words as they are spoken.** A teacher reported late
  subtitles. That class's logs: 95 teacher lines translated, median 14
  characters, 90th percentile 111, longest 499 (about half a minute); the
  translate route answered in 0.1–0.3 s bar outliers of 1.9 s and 2.7 s. The
  wait was the finished-utterance rule, so partials now show untranslated.
