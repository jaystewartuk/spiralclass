# D-144. The student checkout stops collecting an address it has no use for, and is reordered around the buyer's decision

**What changed.**

Two things, both on the public funnel (`/b/[slug]` and `/b/[slug]/buy`), neither
touching how money moves.

1. **`billing_address_collection` on the STUDENT rail now follows
   `STRIPE_TAX_ENABLED` instead of being hardcoded `"required"`.** With tax off —
   which is every deploy today — Stripe collects only what the chosen payment
   method itself needs, typically a postcode and country on a card, instead of
   country, address line 1, city, state and postcode. The teacher's own
   subscription checkout (`createSubscriptionCheckoutSession`) is deliberately
   unchanged: that is the platform's own UK sale, where the address is a VAT
   record rather than readiness.
2. **The buy page is one narrow column ordered by the buyer's decision
   sequence** — who and what, then pay, then reassurance, then other rails, with
   the class-time picker before the pay button when it is required and after it
   when it is optional. It was a two-column grid whose first control was the
   payment-rail switcher. Alongside that: the chosen package collapses to a
   summary line with a Change affordance, the teacher's photo and one published
   testimonial and the 24-hour cancellation promise now appear at checkout, slot
   times render in the VISITOR's timezone with the teacher's named beside them,
   the chosen day is spelled out in words, the pay button's price goes through
   `formatPriceForBuyer` like the rest of the funnel, and an approximate USD
   figure appears beside the teacher's own currency on a booking page she has
   chosen to sell in English.

**Why it likely changed.**

Because the card rail became real on 2026-08-30 and nobody had ever bought
through it.

The measurement that prompted this: `booking_page_viewed` carries a
`stripeReady` flag, and it is **false on every non-owner view** from the event's
introduction on 2026-07-29 until roughly 20:40 on 2026-08-30, when D-143's work
landed and the teacher's account went chargeable. Every payment in the
product's history arrived on the Wise rail in pesos. So "zero card payments
ever" is not evidence about the card checkout; it
is evidence that no visitor was ever offered one. That makes this pre-launch
work on a path whose first real buyer had not yet arrived, and it is why none of
it is justified by a conversion delta: there is no card cohort to compare, the
same absence [D-137](./D-137.md) recorded when it looked at this funnel.

What each change answers:

- **The address.** `stripe/tax.ts` described a deliberate "capture-now,
  enable-later" posture: bank an address on every payment so that flipping
  `STRIPE_TAX_ENABLED` later needs no backfill. The reasoning was sound and the
  cost was invisible, because it was paid by students rather than by the
  codebase — five fields on the highest-abandonment screen in the product, for a
  registration that does not exist. Tax is computed from the address collected
  at the time of a charge, so a past sale is never taxed by a later
  registration; what the old posture actually bought was completeness of
  historical rows, and that is not worth a field wall in front of someone
  buying a Spanish class.
- **The order.** The rail switcher led the page, so a stranger who had just
  decided to spend MX$1,300 was asked to reason about Stripe versus SPEI before
  seeing what they were confirming — and its default was Card, which this
  teacher could not accept for the whole period the page has existed. The
  package list was asked a second time after the landing page's `?package=`
  link had already collected the answer: `package_selected` fires only on a
  genuine change and **has never fired once**. And the grid stacked the page's
  own `<h1>` below the content it titled on a phone, with the pay button under
  everything.
- **The reassurance.** Every reason a visitor decided to buy — her face, her
  voice, the testimonials, the free-cancellation promise — lived on the landing
  page and none of it followed them to checkout. The moment of maximum doubt was
  the one screen carrying nothing.
- **The timezone.** "Times shown in your teacher's zone (America/Mexico_City)"
  beside a native date input reading `01/09/2026` asks a buyer abroad — which is
  this teacher's entire stated audience — to do both timezone and date-format
  arithmetic mid-checkout, on a step that is optional for a package and
  therefore free to abandon.
- **The second price.** `MX$6,000` tells a buyer in Chicago nothing, and they
  must decide whether to keep reading before they can find out. Charging in USD
  was rejected outright: under [D-143](./D-143.md) her Stripe account settles in
  Mexico in pesos, SPEI is peso-only, and `pricing_currency` is capture-once
  ([D-64](./D-64.md)), so a switch would charge students in a currency her own
  account does not settle in and strand her existing prices. The number is
  presentation; the charge is unchanged.

**What was affected.**

- `lib/payments/start-checkout.ts` passes `billingAddressCollection`;
  `lib/stripe/tax.ts`'s contract comment is rewritten, since it described the
  opposite. `lib/stripe/client.ts` is untouched — its `?? "required"` default
  still governs the subscription path.
- `b/[slug]/buy/purchase-flow.tsx` rewritten; `buy/page.tsx` selects one
  published testimonial and the photo and narrows the container;
  `buy/checkout-form.tsx` switches formatter; `buy/class-slot-picker.tsx` gains
  the viewer-zone resolution and the long date.
- New `lib/pricing/approx-usd.ts`. It reuses **`EconomicsAssumptions`** — the
  operator-maintained singleton behind the P&L, carrying `fxAsOf` — rather than
  introducing a rates feed or a hardcoded table. `money-metrics.ts`'s standing
  "this app has no FX-rate source" remains true in the sense it means: nothing
  calls a rates API.
- Three new catalog keys across `en` / `es-MX` / `fr`, plus
  `web.buyFlow.timesInYourZone` and `web.buyFlow.approxPrice`.
- `web.buyFlow.wisePriceWithSaving` is now unused: the transfer saving moved
  from the individual package rows to the disclosure summary, where it shows
  whether or not the list is expanded.

**Unresolved risks.**

- **The Stripe step itself is still unexercised.** No test purchase was made, so
  what Embedded Checkout actually renders after `billing_address_collection`
  changes — and whether `customer_email` fully suppresses a second email field,
  which the accompanying review initially assumed it did not — is read from the
  API contract, not observed. D-143 already carries the sibling gap: that OXXO
  surfaces and that Adaptive Pricing engages are both unverified.
- **The approximate USD figure couples a public price hint to an internal
  accounting input.** An admin setting a conservative rate for the P&L moves
  what buyers see. Mitigated by refusing to render past 60 days of staleness and
  by labelling it approximate, not by separating the two concerns.
- **The `en`-funnel-locale gate is a proxy.** A teacher selling in English to an
  audience that thinks in euros or sterling still gets dollars. Chosen over
  visitor IP because IP is actively misleading for this teacher — her buyers are
  English speakers living in Mexico — but it is a proxy either way.
- **A partial address is now the norm on student payments.** Rows taken while
  tax is off keep whatever the payment method required and nothing more, so
  turning `STRIPE_TAX_ENABLED` on cannot retroactively complete them.
- **None of this is measured, and cannot be yet.** There is no card cohort. The
  next real card checkout is the first data point, and `checkout_submitted`
  against `checkout_started` is where to look for it.
