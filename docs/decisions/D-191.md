# D-191. A teacher's money screens lead with what she earned this month, not with a "safe to spend" average (2026-10-01)

**Status.** Active.

**What changed.** The dashboard tile and the top of the Payments page now lead
with **what she has earned teaching so far this month**. Under it:

- what the rest of the month adds, from classes already booked on paid packages;
- **last month, closed and named** ("September");
- **"paid in advance"**: money students have paid for classes she has still to
  teach.

"Safe to spend each month" is gone: the label, the 3-month trailing average
under it, and its "provisional" warm-up states.

"Earned by month" lists every month she has taught, up to 12, newest first.
Each month opens to the students it was taught to, and those rows add up to
the month exactly. The list also carries:

- the total so far this calendar year;
- a **typical month and her lowest month**, stated as facts about her own
  history. They are computed over complete months only: not the current month,
  and not her first.

A no-show now counts as earned in its month. It was already earned in the
all-time earned/held split, per [D-12](./D-12.md).

**Why.** A teacher who had moved her books off a paper notebook asked, on the
morning of 1 October, how much she had closed September with and how it
compared with August. None of the screens could say:

- "This month" had reset.
- The month-by-month history did not exist.
- The hero, "Para gastar cada mes", was a 3-month average. She used that label
  as the name of the whole box, not as an answer to anything.

Three things settled it:

1. **No earnings product leads with an average.** Preply, Outschool, Lyft,
   Uber, DoorDash, Fiverr, Upwork and Stripe's Express dashboard all lead with
   a calendar-period total of money actually earned. Outschool and Etsy compare
   by month and year. A projection as headline appears only at Substack
   ("gross annualized revenue"), and it is criticised for reading higher than
   what reaches the bank.
2. **"Safe to spend" already means something else.** Simple, Monzo ("left to
   spend") and Copilot ("free to spend") use the phrase for **cash on hand
   minus the bills you have coming**. We know neither her balance nor her
   bills. Under [D-143](./D-143.md) the money is in her own account, not ours.
   A revenue average under that label invites a weak month to be overspent.
3. **Freelancer guidance smooths over a year and budgets from the lowest
   month, not a 3-month mean** (Found, Lunch Money). So the history now states
   both, plainly, as facts about her own months.

Measured, for the weight to give the screen: `/payments` was opened 60 times
in the 60 days to 1 October, all by one teacher. That is PostHog `$pageview`
on spiralclass.com. Her last visit before the voice note was six minutes
before it.

**What was considered and rejected.**

- **Keep the average and rename it** ("average month"). As the hero it still
  answers a question nobody asked first, and a partial current month sits
  beside a smoothed one. It survives only as the "typical month" line under the
  history.
- **Compare this month so far with last month's total.** That compares a
  part-month with a whole one, which makes the first three weeks of every
  month look like a slump. Last month is shown closed, beside the hero, not as
  a percentage against it.
- **Project the month from the average.** A forecast, however labelled, is
  read as a promise. Booked classes are real and named as booked.
- **"Held", "deferred" or "unearned".** No seller-facing product uses them. In
  SpiralClass nobody holds the money: she has it, and what she owes is
  classes. Upwork's "work in progress" and Fiverr's "active orders" name the
  stage, not the accounting.
- **A net-of-fees figure.** That is forbidden by [D-152](./D-152.md): the
  platform never sees Stripe's fee, so it cannot state one. The amounts are
  labelled as what students paid.

**What it constrains.**

- Do not put a spending recommendation, "safe" figure or forecast on the
  teacher's money screens without knowing her commitments. What these screens
  state is what happened and what is booked.
- Every monthly figure (this month, last month, the history rows, the student
  rows, the year so far and the typical month) is one figure, with one set of
  rules in `docs/features/payments.md`. A new view of earnings uses those rules
  or it shows the same month two ways.

**What was affected.**

- `apps/web/src/lib/cashflow.ts`:
  - `safeMonthlySpendCents` and `provisionalMonths` removed;
  - `bookedRestOfMonth*`, `yearToDateCents`, `typicalMonth` and
    `MonthEarnings.byStudent` added;
  - `apportion()` added;
  - no-shows counted.
- `apps/web/src/components/cashflow-summary.tsx`:
  - `EarningsTile` replaces `SafeToSpendTile`;
  - `EarningsSummary` replaces `CashFlowPanel`;
  - `EarningsByMonth` gains per-student rows.
- The `web.cashflow.*` catalog block in en, es and fr. The unused legacy
  `cashflow.*` keys were deleted.
- The dead `CashFlowSummary` wire type in `packages/shared/src/api.ts`, left
  behind by the mobile API's removal, was deleted.
- `/demo`'s fixture.
- `docs/features/payments.md`.

**Trade-offs accepted, and unresolved risks.**

- **No downloadable statement yet.** A monthly and yearly CSV or PDF for an
  accountant is what Lyft, Uber, Patreon and Stripe all provide, and it is the
  next thing this screen needs. The payments CSV covers money received, not
  money earned.
- **No same-month-last-year comparison.** The history stops at 12 months, so
  it cannot show last September beside this one.
- **"Booked for the rest of the month" can fall.** A cancellation or a
  student's no-show changes it. It is labelled as booked, not expected.
- **The research is desk research.** The "paid in advance" wording and the
  typical/lowest line have not been tried with a teacher. The teacher who
  asked is the cheapest test.
